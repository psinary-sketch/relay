## §1 — THE SUPPLIER/COMPRESSION SPLIT

The type audit produced an apparent tension: R4 is the register the arc keeps arriving at
(cheapest sufficient, lowest type) **and** the register W-LI face 2 showed cannot carry the
wall. **The tension dissolves once two questions the corpus has been treating as one are
separated:**

- **R4 AS A SUPPLIER ROUTE — BARRED.** A criterion is not a carrier. λ_n ≥ 0 tests a
  configuration; it does not exhibit the positive pair-form the wanted poster names. W-LI face
  2 measured this precisely: the register's statistic prices distance-to-truth and degenerates
  identically under RH, which is the signature of a test rather than an object.
- **R4 AS A COMPRESSION ROUTE — OPEN.** No certified barrier bars a finite-to-all bound here,
  and the reason it is even askable is structural: **the compression shape requires an
  enumerated family, and only a Π₁-native register provides one.** R2's index set is not
  computable; R1, R3, R5 quantify over totalities. R4 alone hands you a sequence you can index
  and check.

**The consequence, stated as the finding:** the arc's cheapest-door result (E-9) and its
lowest-type register (this audit) coincide **because both track ENUMERABILITY, not because
either supplies positivity.** Enumerability is what makes a register cheap to test and what
makes it type-low; positivity is a different property, and R4 has the first without the
second. **So the coincidence that looked like convergence on a solution is convergence on a
shared prerequisite — and the split says which further question each half licenses:** the
supplier question is closed at R4 (barred), the compression question is open at R4 (and only
there).
