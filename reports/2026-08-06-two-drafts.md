## DRAFT 1 — THE KNIFE-EDGE CRITERION (closes the define-or-retire work-order if ruled in)

**The criterion: the CERTIFIED REGION'S POSITION RELATIVE TO THE OBJECT.**

| cell | kind | the pencil | certified region | object's position |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **AT** | selection-kind | **c-pencil** (the toy's Gleason direction) | confinement certified **exactly on the extremal stratum**, with exact failure off it | **on** the certified region |
| **BOUNDARY** | zero-margin-kind | **t-pencil** (de Bruijn–Newman) | Λ_dBN ≥ 0, a theorem | **on the edge** — RH ⟺ Λ = 0 |
| **AWAY** | complement-kind | **h-pencil** (Lagarias differenced family) | unconditional on **\|h\| ≥ ½** | **in the complement**, at h = 0 |

**REFUSAL CLAUSE (drafted with it):** *a pencil files only with **its certified region AND the
object's position** both stated.* A pencil that names a parameter and a wall without saying where
the certification lives is not classifiable and is refused.

**What it decides, which is the test of whether it is a criterion at all.** It answers the
question left undecided at F.2026-08-05-r: **the three pencils fall in three different cells, so
they are THREE INSTANCES, not one fact re-encountered** — and the inverted orientation stops being
an anomaly and becomes **two cells of a three-valued scheme** (AT and AWAY), which is what a
criterion is supposed to do with an anomaly.

### The draft's chief weakness, stated rather than left for the ruling to find

**IT WAS DRAFTED FROM THE THREE INSTANCES IT CLASSIFIES, AND THEY SATURATE IT EXACTLY — three
pencils, three cells, no cell empty and no instance unplaced.** A taxonomy fitted to its whole
sample has no out-of-sample evidence, and a three-cell scheme that sorts three objects into three
cells is the shape a fitted taxonomy takes.

**The test that would earn it, proposed with the draft: the next pencil the corpus meets must be
classified BEFORE its cell is known** — its certified region and object position read off the
source, the cell assigned, and only then compared with how the pencil actually behaves. **Until
that runs, the criterion is a description of three cases and should be ruled on as one.**

**IF RULED AGAINST:** the phrase **"the knife-edge anatomy" retires**, §330 is cited directly, and
each pencil is described in its own words. That option is live and costs nothing already built.

## DRAFT 2 — BURIAL'S KIND COLUMN (one-line amendment)

**The amendment.** Faces record their **construction kind — synthetic / real-object.** Matching
kinds are **not required** for the exponent test; **the column is mandatory.** Agreement across
kinds **strengthens** a law; disagreement **localizes** the failure to the construction rather
than to the law.

**Against the head's existing criterion** — *height reached, depth required, measured or derived
relation* — this adds a fourth column and changes no admission rule.

**The column as it would stand today:**

| face | height / depth / relation | kind |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **the Li/√n law** (the one admitted face) | γ / n ≈ 5.9·γ²/δ / exponent 2 | **derived** twice (Cayley detector law; Lagarias `S_fin(n) = λₙ(√n)`), **measured once — SYNTHETIC** (400 on-line ordinates with a quadruple injected) |

### The consequence worth stating BEFORE the run returns, because it constrains what the run can do

**Under this amendment the validation's eventual height–depth pair would still NOT be a second
face.** It measures the **same statistic** (λₙ) in the **same relation** (depth ∝ γ²/δ) as the
admitted face; only the **constant** differs, because the Epstein object's background is
`S∞ ~ n log n` where R4's was bounded. **Same relation, same face.**

**So the run would supply a REAL-OBJECT measurement of the one existing face — a cross-kind
confirmation — and BURIAL would remain proposed-with-one-face whatever the run returns.** That is
consistent with the standing registration (*no promotion follows from the run itself*) and sharper
than it: **the run cannot promote BURIAL even in principle**, because it cannot produce a second
face, only a second kind of measurement of the first.

**That is the amendment's actual payoff and the reason it is worth ruling on now rather than
after:** without the kind column, a real-object measurement arriving after a synthetic one is easy
to mistake for a second face. With it, the two are visibly the same face measured twice — and the
cross-kind agreement, if it comes, is the strongest thing a single face can carry.
