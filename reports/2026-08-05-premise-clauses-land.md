## §2 — THE RESOLVABILITY TEST REPLACES THE OCCURRENCE PROXY (filed as I-8)

> **A term is HELD when its STATEMENT appears in the mirror.**

**Why name-frequency is not evidence, and runs the wrong way.** **Repetition is what an
unresolvable name does.** A term that has been stated is stated once and pointed at thereafter; a
term never stated must be re-named at every use, because there is nothing to point at. **High
name-count is therefore weak evidence AGAINST holding** — the opposite of what the proxy assumed.

### The screen's own limit, found by spot-checking it and filed with the test

The automated screen passed all five. **Hand spot-checking found one of those passes false.** The
content markers that cleared `non-discriminating by design` matched generic phrases in unrelated
files.

over-credit, in opposite directions.** The missing condition is the same:

> **the statement must be findable FROM the mention — co-located, or explicitly pointed at.**

A screen without locality measures whether the corpus contains the words, not whether a reader can
get from the name to the thing. **Filed with I-8 rather than discovered later.**
