## (1) T — named, finite, Euler-product-excluded by construction

Six tools. For each: the checkable property, and the quantity it computes. **T contains no Euler product** —
that is the honest restriction established last pass (`reports/2026-07-29-face-E-two-witness.md`): if the
Euler product were in T, ξ and Z would be T-distinguishable and the barrier vacuous.

| tool | checkable property | quantity it computes |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **T1 functional equation** | ∃ a reflective FE F(s) = F(1−s) (self-dual completion), centring the critical line at σ=1/2 | the s ↔ 1−s symmetry; the location of the centre |
| **T2 analytic continuation** | meromorphic continuation to ℂ, single simple pole at s=1 (removed by the s(s−1) factor in the completion) | the values of F(s) off the region of absolute convergence; the pole datum |
| **T3 order-1 growth / Hadamard** | the completed function is entire of order 1; Hadamard product of genus 1 | the growth envelope; hence the zero-counting asymptotic |
| **T4 Dirichlet series** | a Dirichlet series Σ a(n) n^(−s), absolutely convergent for σ>1, real coefficients a(n) | the coefficient sequence as an abstract Dirichlet series (no factorization asserted) |
| **T5 zero-counting N(T)** | Riemann–von Mangoldt asymptotic N(T) ∼ (T/2π) log(T/2π) − T/2π + O(log T) | the number of zeros to height T |
| **T6 codimension-2 transversality (FOCUS)** | F real-valued on σ=1/2 (from T1 + real coefficients), so off-line zeros require Re F = Im F = 0 (two real conditions) | the codimension of an off-line zero (= 2) |

T is expressive — it is essentially the FE-and-growth analytic toolkit — yet finite and explicit. The one
structural feature it deliberately omits is the multiplicative one (the Euler product / the σ>1 zero-free
region it supplies).

## (3) Clause (ii) — DIVERGENCE (closed by literature)

**Davenport–Heilbronn (1936)**, *On the zeros of certain Dirichlet series*, J. London Math. Soc. **11**,
181–185 (part I) & 307–312 (part II): the Epstein zeta function of a positive-definite binary quadratic form
whose discriminant has class number h ≥ 2 has infinitely many zeros in the half-plane σ > 1 — hence off the
critical line. For **disc −23, h = 3 > 1**, this applies to ξ_Q. (See also Voronin 1975; Bombieri–Hejhal
1995 for refinement to positive proportion.) The discriminant chosen carries the result: h(−23)=3 is standard
(PARI `quadclassunit(-23).no = 3`; corpus CLASS_NUMBER_ANOMALY), and the programme independently **located**
two simple off-line zeros of this exact ξ_Q by argument-principle winding (`0.953+16.29i`, `0.798+29.55i`;
INSTRUMENTS.md, F.2026-07-27).

So the **positivity property P = "all nontrivial zeros lie on σ=1/2"** (equivalently the Li/Hankel
definiteness) is **provably FALSE for Z = ξ_Q**, while conjecturally true for ξ. The two witnesses **diverge on P**.

## (4) The assembled theorem (paper form, each clause's status attached)

> **Theorem (Face E, Tier 1 — the Euler-product barrier against T).**
> Let T = {T1 functional equation, T2 analytic continuation, T3 order-1 growth / Hadamard factorization,
> T4 Dirichlet-series expansion, T5 Riemann–von Mangoldt counting, T6 codimension-2 transversality} — the
> finite analytic toolkit above, **excluding the Euler product**. Let ξ be the completed Riemann zeta and
> Z = ξ_Q the completed Epstein zeta of the principal form of discriminant −23 (h=3). Then:
>
> (i) [PROVED-FOR-BOTH, §2] ξ and Z **agree** on every property in T;
> (ii) [PROVED, Davenport–Heilbronn 1936, §3] the positivity P fails for Z (Z has off-line zeros), while P
>      is the target for ξ;
> (iii) [DERIVES from (i)+(ii), §4b] therefore **no derivation using only T establishes P for ξ**.
>
> Equivalently (contrapositive): **any derivation of the positivity for ξ must use a tool outside T — and the
> tool T omits is the Euler product.** This is the Euler/C₄-essentiality moral (SIMPLICITY_OF_RIEMANN_ZEROS,
> rail; CONTROLLED_EXPERIMENT) stated as a theorem against a specified method class.

### (4b) How it instantiates `invariance_barrier` — and the precision this forces

The committed lemma is `invariance_barrier : agree x y → ¬(P x ↔ P y) → ¬ DeterminedBy agree P`, where
`DeterminedBy agree P := ∀ x y, agree x y → (P x ↔ P y)`. Instantiating the **raw** lemma at (ξ, Z) requires
`¬(P ξ ↔ P Z)`. Since P(Z) is false (DH), `¬(P ξ ↔ P Z) ⟺ P ξ ⟺ RH` — so the raw `¬DeterminedBy` form
**would require knowing RH**, and there is no proven-positive partner available (no Euler-product-free
Dirichlet series is *known* to have all zeros on the line). **This is a genuine precision of the pass:** the
Tier-1 theorem is not "T does not *determine* P" (that needs a true-P witness) but "**T does not *derive*
P(ξ)**," and it uses the **soundness corollary** of the schema, not the raw form.

Model a T-derivation of P at x as `DerivesP_T x := ∀ z, agree_T x z → P z` (a T-proof, using only T-accessible
facts, transports to every T-agreeing z — this is soundness/relativization). Then, from `agree_T ξ Z` (clause
i) and `¬ P Z` (clause ii):
```
DerivesP_T ξ → P Z    (apply to z := Z)      contrapositive with ¬P Z:   ¬ DerivesP_T ξ.
```
`¬ DerivesP_T ξ` is the honest conclusion (iii) — one line, axiom-free, no appeal to RH. It is the **sibling**
of `invariance_barrier` (same skeleton: a property invariant on an agreement relation cannot separate agreeing
points). **Flag for a possible kernel ruling (no commit this pass):** the manuscript's operative terminal is
this derivability corollary `derivability_barrier : agree x y → ¬ P y → ¬ DerivesP_T x`, a one-line companion
to the committed `invariance_barrier`. Whether to add it as a v1.7 sibling is the author's call; the present
report uses it only at the manuscript level. This resolves and sharpens last pass's framing (*"cannot certify
definiteness for one while it fails for the other"* was already the derivability reading).

## (5) Novelty calibration (mandatory, honest)

**The mathematics is ~90-year-old folklore.** The Epstein/Davenport–Heilbronn example as the demonstration
that "the functional equation alone is insufficient, the Euler product is essential" is standard and widely
stated:

- **Davenport–Heilbronn (1936)** — the off-line zeros themselves.
- **Potter–Titchmarsh (1935)**, and **Titchmarsh**, *The Theory of the Riemann Zeta-Function* (§ on Epstein
  counterexamples) — the moral in textbook form.
- **Bombieri–Hejhal (1995)**, **Voronin (1975)** — refinements (positive proportion off-line; universality).
- **Bombieri**'s Clay Millennium problem description and many surveys — "RH is expected only for L-functions
  with an Euler product; Epstein zetas show the FE does not suffice." The **model-theoretic-separation**
  reading is even in the corpus's own monograph (A_Place_to_Stand L603) and the rail SIMPLICITY paper.

So the **example, the moral, and the "Euler product is essential" slogan are all known**. Even the *idea* that
a proof of RH must "use the Euler product essentially / cannot relativize to general Dirichlet series" is
implicit in the folklore.

**What is genuinely new (candidate, honestly scoped):**
1. **The precise specification of T** — pinning the folklore's vague "functional-equation-only methods" to an
   explicit finite list of six checkable tools, each verified PROVED-FOR-BOTH for a named witness. The folklore
   asserts insufficiency; it does not enumerate the method class.
2. **The barrier / relativization framing** — casting it in the Baker–Gill–Solovay oracle-separation shape, so
   that "no T-derivation establishes RH for ξ" is a *stated theorem with a named method class*, not a moral.
3. **The machine-checked schema** — `InvarianceBarrier.invariance_barrier` (axiom-free, v1.6), the abstract
   skeleton the argument instantiates, checked by compiler.

**Framing of the contribution (as ruled):** this is **formalizing folklore into a stated theorem**, *not a
discovery*. The value is in the precision (a finite, checkable T; a stated theorem; a machine-checked
skeleton), not in the mathematical content, which is classical and universally credited to
Davenport–Heilbronn and the tradition after them.
