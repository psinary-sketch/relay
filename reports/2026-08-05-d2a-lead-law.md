## THE SHARED FIELD LAYER — SCOPED (a scoping pass, not a build)

D-2b's confinement certificates and D-2a's un-derived link need the same object: **ℚ(√q) with
its Galois conjugation** — the field the genus ≤ 5 sitting used computationally (the
Galois-locked H(u) ∈ ℚ[s²] with Sturm over ℚ, cross-checked against ℚ(√2) certificates;
**our own prior work**, cited from relay `2026-08-02-extremality-audit-genus5-keystone.md`,
not imported from outside practice).

**What Mathlib supplies.** `Mathlib.NumberTheory.Zsqrtd` (Basic, ToReal, GaussianInt,
QuadraticReciprocity) gives ℤ[√d] with its ring structure, conjugation, norm, and an embedding
to ℝ — the arithmetic spine. The Galois/field-theory library supplies the general apparatus
(`FieldTheory.Galois`, adjoin-root, separability) from which ℚ(√q) and its order-2
automorphism are available, at the cost of instantiating rather than inventing.

**What the module must build.** Two things, and they are the whole cost: (1) the **ordered**
structure on ℚ(√q) — sign decisions for a + b√q, which the genus-5 instrument implemented by
hand (compare a² and qb² with case analysis on signs) and which Mathlib does not package for
this use; (2) **Sturm-chain root counting — Mathlib has no Sturm development at all** (checked:
no occurrence anywhere in the library), so the confinement certificates' decision procedure
must be built from scratch or replaced. **The honest re-price of D-2b follows from (2):** the
computational proof used Sturm chains; a kernel proof either builds Sturm (a substantial
development in its own right) or takes a different route to the same conclusion — for the
three genus-≤5 objects the polynomials are explicit and of low degree, so **exact rational
root-location by `decide`-style case analysis on explicit factorizations is the cheaper
route**, and it is the one D-2b should take.

**Does building the layer convert D-2a's stipulation, as registered?** **Partly, and the
registered expectation is HALF-CONFIRMED — stated plainly rather than rounded up.** With
ℚ(√q) in hand, the top-coefficient identification `c g = ptTop` becomes derivable, because the
certificate's coefficients p(i)/(√q)^i become expressible and the top one evaluates to
p(2g)/q^g — so **stipulation (2) converts.** Stipulation (1), Duursma's functional equation,
does **not** convert at any field cost: it is a theorem about codes, not about fields, and
re-deriving it would mean formalizing weight enumerators, MacWilliams duality, and the zeta
construction — a different and much larger programme. **So: one of two stipulations converts,

either way.** The layer is genuinely shared — built once, it serves D-2b's certificates and
