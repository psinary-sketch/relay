## §2 — THE EMPTY-REPAIR CATEGORY, FILED

> **A check installed EARLY produces no repair, and the absence IS the evidence it worked** — as
> distinct from a check installed LATE, whose yield is damage found.

**The navigator-coinage check's first cycle is the corpus's first recorded instance of the
former.** Three phrases probed, all three found unminted, and every one of their eleven
occurrences turned out to be a *mention* inside the entries discussing them rather than a live
anchor. **Nothing needed fixing, and that was the result.**

**The asymmetry, which is the reason the category needs a name: the second kind is
under-represented in every record, because a check that prevents damage leaves nothing behind
unless the closing act names it.** A ledger that records only repairs will show early checks as
worthless and late ones as valuable — exactly backwards.

**Standing instruction: when a check runs clean, close it by recording that it ran clean and what
it would have caught.**

**And this pass is the contrast case in the same breath**, which is why the two file together:
the coinage check ran early and found nothing to repair; the resolvability standard ran *late* —
against rows landed yesterday — and found three rows already in the condition it forbids, two of
which remain in it. **Same corpus, same day, both kinds of yield.**
