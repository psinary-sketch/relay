## 6. The corollary and what it says

The zero of §5 is one fact about Z_Q0. Read through the schema it has three consequences: one on the Li coefficients, one on the zero side of the explicit formula, and one on its arithmetic side. Each is a theorem that binds no premise.

**On the Li coefficients.** Not every Li coefficient of the configuration of Λ_Q's zeros is nonnegative (`zq_li_nonneg_fails`). This is §4 (3) read backwards. Nonnegativity of every coefficient would put every zero on the line, and one zero is off it.

**On the zero side.** There is a test function k in classK at which the zero side has negative real part,

  Re Σ_ρ m(ρ) k̂(γ_ρ) < 0,  where γ_ρ = (ρ − 1/2)/i and k̂(z) = ∫ k(u) e^{izu} du,

the sum running over the zeros of Λ_Q with their multiplicities. classK is the class of test functions the criterion uses: k is even, twice continuously differentiable and compactly supported, and k = h ⋆ h̃ for some h that is twice continuously differentiable and compactly supported, where h̃(u) is the complex conjugate of h(−u).

The statement comes from a theorem about any configuration (`wide_detector`). Let Z be a set of points in the strip −1/2 < Re s < 3/2, each with a multiplicity, closed under ρ ↦ 1 − ρ̄ with equal multiplicities, and with at most A₀ log(|t| + 3) points of ordinate in (t, t + 1] for every real t. If one point of Z lies off the line Re s = 1/2, then some k in classK makes the zero side of Z negative in real part.

The proof is a change of scale. The criterion's schema proves the same statement for configurations in the closed strip 0 ≤ Re s ≤ 1 (`detector`), and a configuration in the wider strip is one of those at half scale. The map s ↦ 1/2 + (s − 1/2)/2 carries −1/2 < Re s < 3/2 onto 0 < Re s < 1. It fixes the line, commutes with the reflection, keeps the multiplicities and halves every γ_ρ. The count passes with the constant 4A₀, because a unit window of halved ordinates is two unit windows of the original ones (`half_count`). A test function k found for the half-scale configuration gives x ↦ 2k(2x) for the original. The dilate is again in classK (`dil_classK`), and its transform at γ is the transform of k at γ/2, so the two zero sides agree term by term (`halfConfig_sum`).

At Z_Q0 each hypothesis is a theorem: the point off the line is §5's, the count is §4 (1).

**On the arithmetic side.** There is a test function k in classK at which

  Re [ P(k) − Σ_n c(n) n^(−1/2) · 2k(log n) + A_Q(k) ] < 0

(`zq_arith_side_re_neg`). Here P is the pole term, c(n) are the coefficients of −Z_Q′/Z_Q, and A_Q(k) = (1/2π) ∫ k̂(r) [2 Re ψ(1/2 + ir) − 2 log(2π/√23)] dr is the archimedean term. It is the same k. The explicit formula of §4 (2) holds for every even, twice continuously differentiable, compactly supported k, so for every k in classK, and it says the bracket equals the zero side.

**What it says.** The criterion for ζ is the inequality

  P(k) − Σ_n Λ(n) n^(−1/2) · 2k(log n) + A(k) ≥ 0 for every k in classK

(`h2_sign`), and it is compiled as equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis (`h2_sign_iff_rh`). The bracket above is that expression written for Z_Q0: c(n) stands where von Mangoldt's Λ(n) stood, and the gamma factor of Λ_Q where ζ's stood. For Z_Q0 it takes a value of negative real part on classK, so the inequality fails there‹WEILNAME›.

It fails for a stated reason: a zero is off the line. The direction of the criterion that turns an off-line zero into a negative value has been run here on a zero of a zeta function, and every step is compiled, from the certified enclosure of the zero in §5 to the sign of the arithmetic side.

The schema has been exercised with both outcomes. With a true conclusion, `pc_online` draws "every point is on the line" at the two-point configuration {1/2 + i, 1/2 − i}, every hypothesis met and every Li coefficient nonnegative (`pc_li_nonneg`); and `zeta_box` certifies a zero of ζ on the line with ordinate between 12 and 16, by the instrument of §5. With a false conclusion, there is this section.

**What the theorem gives and what it leaves open.** The theorem gives existence. The k it produces is a window built from the zero: a list of coefficients and a power j, with support growing as 2^j times a width fixed by ρ₀ (`detector`). Neither the list nor j is named. The same theorem applies to each further zero of Z_Q0 off the line once that zero is enclosed in Lean; 68 more are certified numerically to height 445 (FINDINGS :11771; relay data/b725_interference.txt).

**What the bench shows of such a k.** Two numerical results stand beside the theorem; neither is compiled. A window designed for the purpose, a cosine-modulated plateau carrying the factor (γ₀² + D²), makes the criterion's quantity negative at Q0, beyond its error bound, for widths from 34 to 60 in the bench's width parameter (*The Findings As They Stand* v0.7 :322). And the window the detector theorem itself builds at this zero has been evaluated on the banked zeros to height 150: its power is j = 38, and its zero side is about −7.57 × 10^39 (INSTRUMENTS :648). The theorem's window is far wider than the designed one.

**Prior work.** The explicit formula and the positivity criterion are Weil's (1952). Li's criterion is Li's (1997), in the form of Bombieri and Lagarias (1999). That Epstein zeta functions of class number above 1 have zeros off the line is classical: Davenport and Heilbronn (1936) for Re s > 1, Voronin (1976) inside the strip, as §5 cites them. The change of scale is elementary.

## 7. The cost law e^{πγ/2} and the instrument

**The law.** To certify a value of Λ_Q at height γ in the strip, the precision needed grows as e^{πγ/2}. The reason is the gamma factor. By Stirling's formula

  |Γ(σ + iγ)| = √(2π) |γ|^(σ − 1/2) e^(−π|γ|/2) (1 + O(1/|γ|)),

so Λ_Q(s) = (√23/2π)^s Γ(s) Z_Q(s) is smaller than Z_Q(s) by that exponential, while the terms of the sums that compute it are not. The value is what remains after cancellation among terms about e^{πγ/2} times its size.

**The figures at the located zero.** At γ = 16.2902 the factor e^{πγ/2} is 1.2972 × 10^11 (relay data/b744_zetabox_plan.txt :134). The measured figure of §5, 1/|Γ(cA)| = 1.4608 × 10^10, is that factor divided by √(2π) γ^(σ − 1/2), which is ‹F1› at σ = 0.95326, as Stirling's formula says (relay data/b759_costlaw.txt). The conditioning of the centre's sum, its largest term over the tolerance it had to meet, is 0.016640 over 6 × 10^(−13), about 2.8 × 10^10 (:137–:138).

**ζ beside it.** For ζ the gamma factor is Γ(s/2), and the exponent halves: e^{πγ/4} is 6.6263 × 10^4 at ζ's lowest zero, γ = 14.1347, against 3.6016 × 10^5 at the control's height (:135–:136). The task differs as well as the exponent. For ζ on the line a zero is certified by a change of sign of a real function between two points, and each value is needed for its sign and nothing more: the relative precision wanted was 6.2 × 10^(−4). For Z_Q0 off the line a zero is certified by a modulus that is small at a centre and bounded below on a whole frontier: 7.3 × 10^(−13) (:138–:139).

**The instrument.** The six steps of §5 are one instrument, and each step answers the law.

- The θ-representation, split at t = 1, writes Λ_Q through sums that converge rapidly.
- Each term is an incomplete gamma value, computed as an exact rational series with a proved remainder.
- The Γ and power factors are enclosed at an explicit order.
- A generator outside the kernel writes the rational certificates; the kernel checks them by `decide`. The trust is in the kernel's check and not in the generator.
- From points to a region, the discrete Cauchy formula interpolates from a circle inside the strip, where bounds drawn from the function's size would have asked for about 10^11 frontier points (§5).
- The minimum-modulus principle turns "small at the centre, larger on the frontier" into a zero.

**Its control on a known case.** The same instrument, pointed at ζ, returns the zero everyone knows where it should be: there is ρ with ζ(ρ) = 0, Re ρ = 1/2 and 12 < Im ρ < 16 (`zeta_box`). The statement is about Mathlib's `riemannZeta`, by the completed zeta split into incomplete-gamma terms and the sign of the real function on the line at t = 12 and t = 16. It is a control on the instrument and adds nothing to what is known of ζ.

**What the law predicts.** The second zero of Z_Q0 off the line, near 0.798 + 29.55 i (phase1.5/method/INSTRUMENTS.md :556), costs more than the zero of §5 by the factor e^{π(γ′ − γ)/2} = ‹F2›, γ′ and γ their two heights (relay data/b759_costlaw.txt). A certificate there needs a different representation or a sharper remainder, and the present instrument's own figures say so in advance.

**The methods note.** The derivation of the law, the general statements of the three objects Mathlib lacks, and the limits of the method are the subject of `phase1.5/method/CERTIFIED_EVALUATION_IN_THE_STRIP_v0_1.md`, a skeleton of nine headings. ## 8. Class number one, multiplicative representation numbers, and the sign of the logarithmic derivative's coefficients

Two properties of ζ's arithmetic side are easy to take for granted: its coefficients are multiplicative, and the coefficients of −ζ′/ζ are nonnegative. Z_Q0 has neither. Both failures are compiled, each beside the statement for ζ that it is the failing case of.

**Class number one.** When the class group is trivial there is one reduced form, and its Epstein zeta function is the Dedekind zeta function up to the number of units: Z_Q(s) = w ζ_K(s) = w ζ(s) L(s, χ_D). Its coefficients divided by w are Σ_{d|n} χ_D(d), which is multiplicative; the function has an Euler product; and the coefficients of −Z_Q′/Z_Q are those of −ζ_K′/ζ_K, a sum of logarithms of norms of prime ideals, taken over prime-ideal powers, hence nonnegative. At class number one the Epstein zeta function has both properties.

**At discriminant −23 the coefficients are not multiplicative.** With r(n) the number of integer pairs (x, y) with x² + xy + 6y² = n,

  r(1)/2 = 1, r(2) = r(3) = 0, r(6) = 4,

so at the coprime pair 2, 3 the product of the values of r(n)/2 is 0 and its value at 6 is 2 (`rQ_two`, `rQ_three`, `rQ_six`, `rQ_half_one`). The function n ↦ r(n)/2 is not multiplicative in Mathlib's sense (`zq_coeff_not_multiplicative`). Multiplicativity is the hypothesis under which Mathlib proves an Euler product, and ζ's coefficients meet it (`zeta_isMultiplicative_control`).

The reason is the class group. The primes 2 and 3 split in K = ℚ(√−23), and their prime ideals lie in the two classes of order 3, so the principal form represents neither 2 nor 3. A product of a prime ideal above 2 with one above 3 is principal when their classes are inverse, which happens for two of the four products, and each principal ideal of norm 6 is counted twice by the two units: r(6) = 4. The principal form counts principal ideals, and classes multiply where counts do not.

**The coefficients of −Z_Q′/Z_Q are not all nonnegative.** Write −Z_Q′(s)/Z_Q(s) = Σ c(n) n^(−s). The c(n) are determined by the r(n) through the recursion

  2c(n) = r(n) log n − Σ_{d|n, d<n} c(d) r(n/d)  (n ≥ 2)

(`cQ_eq`), and the kernel computes them in closed form along the divisors of 48:

| n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 24 | 48 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| c(n) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 log 2 | 2 log 6 | 6 log 2 | 2 log 12 | 2 log 2 | 0 | −8 log 2 − 2 log 3 |

The last value is −2 log 48 ≈ −7.7424 (`cQ_forty_eight`), so the c(n) are not all nonnegative (`cQ_not_nonneg`), where the von Mangoldt function is (`vonMangoldt_nonneg_control`).

**What the sign bears on.** In the explicit formula the prime-power side is Σ c(n) n^(−1/2) · 2k(log n). For ζ every weight is nonnegative. For Z_Q0 the weights change sign, and §6 shows the whole arithmetic side going negative on classK.

What is proved about products and zeros is a statement about the half-plane Re s > 1: a function with an absolutely convergent Euler product has no zero there, and an Epstein zeta function of class number above 1 has infinitely many (Davenport and Heilbronn). Inside the strip, across the families the programme has tested, an Euler product is present where the zeros stay on the line and absent where they leave it (*Which Structure Confines*). For ζ itself, that the product holds the zeros to the line is the Riemann Hypothesis.

### 9.7 Glossary

- **Configuration.** A set of complex numbers with multiplicities, closed under ρ ↦ 1 − ρ̄, with finitely many in each window of ordinates. Here: the zeros of Λ_Q0 with their orders.
- **classK.** Test functions k that are even, twice continuously differentiable, compactly supported, and of the form h ⋆ h̃.
- **Zero side, arithmetic side.** The two sides of the explicit formula: the sum of k̂ over the configuration, and pole term less prime-power side plus archimedean term.
- **Li coefficient.** Classically λ_n = Σ_ρ [1 − (1 − 1/ρ)^n] over the zeros. The kernel uses the paired form over a configuration: half the sum, with multiplicity, of the real part of the term that pairs ρ with its reflection (`Wide.liCoeff`, Schema/Wide/Converse.lean :32).
- **Premise-free.** A statement that binds no hypothesis of the schema; the schema's premises enter as theorems.
- **The standard three.** The axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound of Lean's library.
- **DERIVES, INTERFACES.** The programme's grades for a compiled statement: it binds no premise of the programme, or it passes a named one through.

## Appendix A. The coefficient law at class number 3: statement and proof

The parts of this proof stand in the record: the general steps in relay data/b718_derivation.txt (steps 1 to 4 and 7), the reason in data/b753_registrations.txt :18–:23, the values in data/b721_derivation.txt. The appendix assembles them at class number 3 and closes the step that b718 named as open there, the bound on the number of prime factors. The table of Step 5 is reproduced by two routes at relay data/b759_c3table.txt.

**Setting.** K is an imaginary quadratic field of class number 3, so its class group C is cyclic of order 3 and it has two units. Q0 is its principal form. Put a(n) = r_Q0(n)/2, the number of principal integral ideals of norm n, so that Z_Q0(s)/2 = Σ a(n) n^(−s) with a(1) = 1, and define b(n) by log(Z_Q0(s)/2) = Σ_{n ≥ 2} b(n) n^(−s). Call a rational prime *bad* when it splits in K and its two prime ideals are not principal, which is to say it splits and Q0 does not represent it; call every other prime *good*.

**Theorem.** Let p1 < p2 < p3 be the three least bad primes. Then b(n) ≥ 0 for every n < n0 = min(p1⁴ p2, p1² p2 p3), and b(n0) = −2.

**Step 1: characters.** C has three characters, 1, ψ and ψ̄. By orthogonality a(n) = (1/3) Σ_χ Σ_I χ(I), the inner sum over the integral ideals I of norm n, so

  Z_Q0(s)/2 = (1/3) [ζ_K(s) + L(s, ψ) + L(s, ψ̄)],

and each of the three is an Euler product over the rational primes.

**Step 2: the local factors.** Write x = p^(−s).
- p inert: the ideals of p-power norm are the powers of (p), all principal. The factor is 1/(1 − x²) for every χ.
- p ramified, (p) the square of a prime ideal: the class of that ideal has order dividing 2 in a group of order 3, so the ideal is principal. The factor is 1/(1 − x) for every χ.
- p split with principal prime ideals: the factor is 1/(1 − x)² for every χ.
- p bad, with prime ideals of classes g and g⁻¹: the factor is 1/(1 − x)² at χ = 1 and 1/((1 − ωx)(1 − ω̄x)) = 1/(1 + x + x²) at χ = ψ and at χ = ψ̄, whichever of the two non-trivial classes g is.

**Step 3: the two factors.** Let G be the product of the local factors at the good primes, the same for every χ. Then Z_Q0/2 = G · H with

  H = (1/3) [ Π_bad (1 − x_p)^(−2) + 2 Π_bad (1 + x_p + x_p²)^(−1) ],

and log(Z_Q0/2) = log G + log H. log G is a sum over the good primes of series in one variable each, −log(1 − x²), −log(1 − x) or −2 log(1 − x), with nonnegative coefficients. log H is a power series in the variables of the bad primes alone, symmetric in them. Three things follow.
- If n is a power of a good prime, b(n) ≥ 0.
- If n is divisible by a good prime and by another prime, b(n) = 0.
- If n = q1^e1 ⋯ qk^ek with every qi bad, then b(n) is the coefficient of x1^e1 ⋯ xk^ek in log H. It depends on the exponents e1, …, ek and not on the primes. Write it β(e1, …, ek).

**Step 4: one bad prime.** In one variable H = (1 − x + x²)/((1 − x)²(1 + x + x²)), and since 1 − x + x² = (1 + x³)/(1 + x) and 1 + x + x² = (1 − x³)/(1 − x),

  log H = log((1 + x³)/(1 − x³)) − log(1 − x²) = Σ_{m ≥ 1} x^(2m)/m + 2 Σ_{k odd} x^(3k)/k.

So β(e) ≥ 0 for every e.

**Step 5: the small patterns.** Let α(e1, …, ek) be the coefficient of the same monomial in H; by Step 2, α = (1/3) [Π (ei + 1) + 2 Π A(ei)], where A(e) = 1, −1, 0 as e ≡ 0, 1, 2 modulo 3. Since α(1) = 0, H − 1 has no term of degree below 2, so (H − 1)^k has none below 2k, and in degree at most 5 the series log H = (H − 1) − (H − 1)²/2 + … gives

  β = 2α − (α ∗ α)/2, where (α ∗ α)(e) = Σ_{0 ≤ d ≤ e} α(d) α(e − d).

| pattern | 1,1 | 2,1 | 3,1 | 2,2 | 1,1,1 | 4,1 | 3,2 | 2,1,1 | 1,1,1,1 |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| α | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 6 |
| α ∗ α | 4 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 4 | 20 | 24 | 20 | 36 |
| β | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 2 | −2 | −4 | −2 | −6 |

**Step 6: every other pattern contains a negative one.** Say a pattern e contains a pattern f when the parts of f can be matched to distinct parts of e that are at least as large. A pattern that contains none of (4,1), (3,2), (2,1,1), (1,1,1,1) has one part; or two parts and is (1,1), (2,1), (3,1) or (2,2); or is (1,1,1). By Steps 4 and 5, β ≥ 0 at each of these. So if b(n) < 0 then n is a product of bad primes whose pattern contains one of the four.

**Step 7: size.** Let n be a product of bad primes.
- If its pattern contains (4,1), then n ≥ q⁴q′ for two distinct bad primes, so n ≥ p1⁴ p2.
- If it contains (2,1,1), then n ≥ p1² p2 p3.
- If it contains (3,2), then n ≥ p1³ p2² > p1⁴ p2.
- If it contains (1,1,1,1), then n ≥ p1 p2 p3 p4 > p1² p2 p3.

Hence b(n) < 0 forces n ≥ n0. The integer n0 has pattern (4,1) or (2,1,1) exactly, and β = −2 at both. ∎

**What the proof uses.** Unique factorisation of ideals; the correspondence between classes of forms and ideal classes; orthogonality of characters; and that there are at least three bad primes, since each ideal class contains infinitely many prime ideals of degree one. It uses no list of fields: it holds at every imaginary quadratic field of class number 3, and the walk of §8 is the theorem observed at sixteen of them.

**What it does not need, and what would compile.** Steps 4 to 6 are statements about one power series in at most four variables, and could be compiled as they stand. Steps 1 to 3 need the class group of an imaginary quadratic field, which the kernel does not hold.

**The law the value belongs to.** Let b(n) = c(n)/log n, the coefficients of log(Z_Q(s)/2). The value above is b(48) = −2, and 48 is where a law observed at class number 3 puts it. The law is observed and not proved.

*The law.* Let K be an imaginary quadratic field of class number 3, and let p1 < p2 < p3 be the three least primes that split in K and are not represented by its principal form. Then the least n with b(n) < 0 is n0 = min(p1⁴ p2, p1² p2 p3), and b(n0) = −2.

The law was registered as a hypothesis before any coefficient was computed, and it held at fourteen fields and at the two control fields −23 and −31, in exact rational arithmetic for n up to 90000 (relay data/b753_registrations.txt :14–:16; data/b755_walk.txt :232–:234). At −23 the three primes are 2, 3 and 13, and n0 = min(48, 156) = 48. The primes are 2 and 3 because −23 ≡ 1 (mod 24): a fundamental discriminant is 1 modulo 8 exactly when 2 splits and 1 modulo 3 exactly when 3 splits. `cQ_forty_eight` is the law's value at −23, compiled.
