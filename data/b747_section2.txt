Its docstring calls each field "stated, not proved" (:133-:136). The five fields are the schema's premises. This section says what each states and which classical statement it corresponds to; what holds at Z_Q0, and where, is §3's. Each field below is quoted as the tree writes it, read as one sentence (its object, its hypothesis, its conclusion), and set beside a classical theorem. **(1) continuation.** `continuation : ZQContinuation zq`, the structure of :80-:86:

```lean
  summable : ∀ s : ℂ, 1 < s.re → LSeriesSummable (fun k => (rQ k : ℂ)) s
  agrees : ∀ s : ℂ, 1 < s.re → zq s = ZQ s
  holomorphic : DifferentiableOn ℂ zq {s : ℂ | s ≠ 1}
  functional_equation : ∀ s : ℂ, 0 < s.re → s.re < 1 →
    qBase ^ (-s) * Complex.Gamma s * zq s = qBase ^ (-(1 - s)) * Complex.Gamma (1 - s) * zq (1 - s)
  real_on_real : ∀ x : ℝ, x ≠ 1 → (zq (x : ℂ)).im = 0
```

Read classically: for the function zq, with no hypothesis beyond its type, the lattice sum Σ r_Q0(k) k^(−s) converges absolutely on Re s > 1 and equals zq there, zq is holomorphic off s = 1, Λ_Q(s) = Λ_Q(1 − s) on 0 < Re s < 1, and zq is real on the real axis off 1. It corresponds to Epstein's continuation and functional equation for the zeta function of a positive definite quadratic form (Epstein, 1903), the route through the theta function of the form and Poisson summation that §1's `thetaU` and `zqPair` follow.

**(2) zeros.** `zeros : ZQZeros zq Z`, the structure of :99-:101:

```lean
  carrier_eq : Z.carrier = LambdaQZeros zq
  mult_order : ∀ ρ ∈ Z.carrier, ∃ g : ℂ → ℂ, AnalyticAt ℂ g ρ ∧ g ρ ≠ 0 ∧ ∀ᶠ z in nhds ρ, zq z = (z - ρ) ^ Z.mult ρ * g z
```

Read classically: for zq and a configuration Z, the points of Z are exactly the zeros of zq other than 0, 1 and −1, −2, … (`LambdaQZeros`, :92-:93), and the multiplicity of each point is its order as a zero, zq being (z − ρ)^m times a function analytic and nonzero at ρ. Because Z is a `WideConfig`, the field also carries the fields of Z as facts about those zeros: they lie in the open strip −1/2 < Re s < 3/2, they are closed under ρ ↦ 1 − ρ̄, and any window of ordinates holds finitely many of them (Config.lean :28-:38). It corresponds to the isolated-zeros theorem for a holomorphic function not identically zero, each zero of finite order. It is joined to Davenport and Heilbronn's theorem (1936) that an Epstein zeta of class number above 1 has zeros with Re s > 1, which is why the strip is the widened one (§1).

**(3) logDeriv.** `logDeriv : ∀ s : ℂ, 3 / 2 ≤ s.re → LSeries (fun n => (c n : ℂ)) s = -(deriv zq s / zq s)` (:140).

Read classically: for zq and the sequence c, on the half-plane Re s ≥ 3/2 the Dirichlet series Σ c(n) n^(−s) equals −zq′(s)/zq(s). It corresponds to von Mangoldt's identity −ζ′(s)/ζ(s) = Σ Λ(n) n^(−s) on Re s > 1, with Λ replaced by the coefficients that the series of −Z_Q0′/Z_Q0 determines. The docstring notes that the field does not write their recursion from r_Q0 (:134); §3's instance takes c to be `cQ` (ZQ/LogDeriv.lean :63-:65), defined by that recursion. The absence of an Euler product bears on this field. ζ_K and L(s, ψ) have Euler products; Z_Q0 does not (§1; relay data/b725_interference.txt :11). For ζ the coefficients of −ζ′/ζ are von Mangoldt's, carried by the prime powers because ζ is a product over primes. For Z_Q0 no product stands behind the series, and the field names c by the series it sums to, not by primes.

**(4) explicitFormula.** `explicitFormula : ZQExplicitFormula Z c`, the structure of :127-:129:

```lean

    zeroSideQ Z k = poleTerm k - primeTermQ c k + archTermQ k
```

Read classically: for the configuration Z and the coefficients c, take any test function k that is even, twice continuously differentiable and compactly supported. The sum over the points of Z, with multiplicity, of k̂ at their ordinates (`zeroSideQ`, :107-:108) then equals the pole term, less Σ_n c(n) n^(−1/2) · 2k(log n) (`primeTermQ`, :112-:113), plus the archimedean integral of k̂ against 2 Re ψ(1/2 + ir) − 2 log(2π/√23) (`archTermQ`, :121-:122; `bracketQ`, :117-:118). It corresponds to Weil's explicit formula (Weil 1952; Bombieri and Lagarias 1999, as the kernel's docstring cites them at :124-:126). As §1 set out, the formula is written here over Λ_Q's own zeros and with c in place of von Mangoldt's Λ.

**(5) count.** `count : ∃ A₀ : ℝ, Wide.HCount Z A₀` (:142), with `HCount` (Config.lean :49-:50):


Read classically: for the configuration Z, some constant A₀ ≥ 1 bounds, for every real t, the number of points of Z with ordinate in (t, t + 1], counted with multiplicity (`N`, Config.lean :46), by A₀ log(|t| + 3). It corresponds to the local form of the Riemann–von Mangoldt count, N(T + 1) − N(T) = O(log T) for ζ. Here the count is asked of a configuration whose points need not lie in the critical strip.
