# One Page: the SIDE Reduction of the Riemann Hypothesis to a Single Located Clause

‹BYLINE›
v2.0, November 2026

**The statement.** Let h be a twice continuously differentiable function on the real line with compact support. Let k = h ∗ h̃ be its autocorrelation, where h̃(x) is the complex conjugate of h(−x), and suppose k is even. Write k̂(z) = ∫ k(u) e^{izu} du, Λ for the von Mangoldt function and ψ = Γ′/Γ. Put

W(k) = k̂(i/2) + k̂(−i/2) − 2 Σ_{n ≥ 1} Λ(n) n^{−1/2} k(log n) + (1/2π) ∫ k̂(r) [Re ψ(1/4 + ir/2) − log π] dr.

By the explicit formula, W(k) is the sum of k̂(γ) over the nontrivial zeros 1/2 + iγ of the Riemann zeta function, counted with multiplicity. The number γ is real exactly when the zero is on the critical line.

**Theorem.** The Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to the statement that W(k) ≥ 0 for every such k.

This is Weil's criterion. Both directions are proved in Lean 4 against Mathlib's own statement of the Riemann Hypothesis, with no hypothesis and no axiom beyond the three that Lean's classical mathematics uses. The inequality "W(k) ≥ 0 for every such k" is the located clause: the one statement to which the Hypothesis reduces. It is open. What is proved is the equivalence.

**The same theorem at Dirichlet L-functions.** For every primitive Dirichlet character χ other than the trivial one, the corresponding inequality, with χ's prime sum and archimedean term and no pole term, holds for every such k exactly when every zero of L(s, χ) in the critical strip has real part 1/2. This is compiled in both directions, as an instance of one theorem over any configuration of zeros that has an explicit formula and a local count.

**Three forms of the clause.** Each is compiled as equivalent to the Hypothesis. Li's criterion: the Li coefficients λ_n of ζ are nonnegative for every n. The Bombieri–Lagarias form: for every n, the limit of the explicit formula's arithmetic side along a named family of test functions is nonnegative. Bounded support: for every L, W(k) ≥ 0 for the k that vanish outside [−L, L].

**Where it fails.** Let Z be the Epstein zeta function of the quadratic form x² + xy + 6y², of discriminant −23. Z has a functional equation and no Euler product. For Z the kernel compiles the explicit formula on the same test functions, a zero off the critical line, and the failure of the corresponding inequality and of Li's nonnegativity. It also compiles what Z lacks beside ζ on the prime side. The coefficients of Z are not multiplicative; the failure is at 6 = 2 · 3. The coefficients of its logarithmic derivative are not all nonnegative; the failure is at 48. The coefficients of ζ are multiplicative, and Λ is nonnegative. The criterion therefore separates the two functions, and any argument for the clause must use a property of ζ that Z does not have.

**What holds without the clause.** The explicit formula itself, for ζ and for every primitive character; λ₁ > 0; and a zero of ζ on the critical line between heights 12 and 16, located in Lean by a change of sign.

**How the clause was located.** The programme reached it by exhaustive enumeration. The specification of the completed zeta function, n² → θ → ξ, decomposes into seven mechanism classes: two from the operations of the integers, three from Ostrowski's classification of the places of the rationals, two from the local and global scales, and none from the interfaces between them. For each class the deposited kernel compiles a condition on a real number σ, and each condition singles out σ = 1/2. These conditions fix the line. They are statements about σ and about no function: the kernel's exclusion of off-line zeros class by class, and the zero of Z off the line, are both compiled and do not meet. The step from the line to the zeros of ξ is the clause above, and it is there, on the prime side, that ζ and Z differ. *The Seven Mechanism Classes of ξ(s)* develops the enumeration; *The Unconditional Surround of ξ*, what holds around the clause; *The Epstein Control Object at Discriminant −23*, the control; *A Place to Stand*, the whole.

**What it opens.** A test for any proposed proof of positivity: it must go through for ζ and fail for Z. An effective form of the criterion: how wide a test function must be to detect a zero at a given height and distance from the line. The same theorem for further L-functions that have an explicit formula and a local count.

## Glossary

- **Autocorrelation.** For a function h, the function h ∗ h̃, where h̃(x) is the complex conjugate of h(−x).
- **Configuration.** A set of complex numbers with multiplicities, taken as the zeros of a function, with a statement of where they should lie.
- **Critical strip, critical line.** The complex numbers with real part between 0 and 1; those with real part 1/2.
- **Explicit formula.** The identity that equates a sum over the zeros of a zeta or L-function with a pole term, a sum over prime powers and an archimedean integral.
- **Local count.** A bound on the number of zeros in each window of unit height.
- **Located clause.** The inequality W(k) ≥ 0 for every test function k of the class above: the statement that the kernel proves equivalent to the Riemann Hypothesis and carries as its one open premise.
- **Mechanism class.** One of the seven ways in which the specification of ξ constrains where a zero can sit.
- **SIDE.** The programme's method: Symmetry, Independence, Determination, Exhaustiveness.
- **The three axioms.** `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, the axioms of classical mathematics in Lean.
