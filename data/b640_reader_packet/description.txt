THE RECORD. Version v1.2.0 of the Zenodo record "A Place To Stand: the SIDE reduction of the Riemann Hypothesis to a single located clause" (concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19675355, a DOI being a Digital Object Identifier), the deposit of A PLACE TO STAND, the research programme whose papers, kernels and ledgers this corpus holds: its principal result "a machine-verified reduction of the Riemann Hypothesis to a single located clause, with that clause carried openly as open". This version replaces the files of v1.1.2 that have moved since that deposit by their current editions, adds the programme's keystone census at v0.6, its table of findings at v0.6 and a mirror of the corpus, and sets out, in this order, what is claimed, what is machine-verified, what the kernels assume and what remains open. Against v1.1.2, no claim is added or withdrawn. Nothing in it states that the Riemann Hypothesis holds.

WHAT IS CLAIMED. RH is the Riemann Hypothesis, as Mathlib states it (RiemannHypothesis): every zero s of the Riemann zeta function that is not a trivial zero −2(n+1) and is not s = 1 has real part 1/2, Mathlib being the library of formalised mathematics for the Lean 4 proof assistant. The located clause: the Riemann Hypothesis holds exactly when the explicit-formula quadratic form Q(k) = pole term − prime sum + archimedean term is non-negative for every admissible test window k, the theorem h2_sign_iff_rh deriving the equivalence with no premise, and the open clause being the sign statement h2_sign itself, which the kernel states, proves equivalent to RH, bounds by its mechanism exclusions, and carries as its one open premise. Here classK is the admissible test windows: functions k on the reals that are even, twice continuously differentiable and compactly supported, and of the form h ⋆ h~ with h twice continuously differentiable and compactly supported; h2_sign is Weil positivity on classK: for every test window k in classK, 0 ≤ P(k) − PR(k) + A(k), the pole term, the prime sum and the archimedean term of the explicit formula; open, and carried as the one open premise; and h2_sign_iff_rh is the compiled theorem h2_sign ↔ RiemannHypothesis: "Weil positivity on classK is Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis, both directions, no hypothesis".

The programme's README, the front page of its corpus repository, states the claim in one sentence: "RH reduced to a single located clause, reduction machine-verified." For the versions v0.17 to v0.25 of the kernel SIDE-explicit-formula it carries two further sentences, quoted whole: "the simplicity clause stated as a Prop beside h2_sign with no compiled edge between them (simplicity_iff, v0.17; positivity_not_imp_simplicity, v0.19); Weil positivity of a sum of configurations exactly when it holds for both parts (productLemma_holds, v0.18) and over a finite family exactly when every member's target holds (family_theorem, v0.21, q = 3 by rfl); λ₁ > 0 at the standard three (liCoeff_one_pos, v0.20), a single rung; the Keiper identity and bounds carried at INTERFACES on seven named obligations; the window family defined with two named obligations, independent of the Epstein premises." and "RH up to the Platt–Trudgian height stated as rh_upto and carried at INTERFACES on its one named premise PlattTrudgianHeight (rh_upto_platt, v0.22); the simple-zero proportion two_thirds cited from its source kernel, with its axioms read there, and not proved in this one (SimpleProportion, v0.23); RH ⟺ the Nyman–Beurling condition carried at INTERFACES on NymanBeurlingPremise (rh_iff_nb, v0.24); Weil positivity for the Dedekind configuration of modulus q exactly when the ζ target holds and GRH_chi holds for every member of the family (dedekind_instance, v0.25; dedekind_three at q = 3), its right-hand side split into the trivial character's term and the family's carried at INTERFACES on TrivialSummandPremise and EulerFactorPremise (dedekind_rhs)." A name written with underscores or with joined capitals, such as h2_sign_iff_rh or PlattTrudgianHeight, is the name of a compiled declaration of the programme's kernels and is defined by its statement there.

Not supportable, unchanged: RH proved; h2_sign proved; λ_n ≥ 0 proved; simplicity proved; GRH reduced for any modulus beyond the family theorem's letter. Here λ_n are Li's coefficients, whose non-negativity for every n is compiled as equivalent to RH; simplicity is the simplicity clause the kernel states as a Prop beside h2_sign (simplicity_iff, v0.17), with no compiled edge between them; GRH_chi is the same statement for the zeros of the L-function of one primitive Dirichlet character χ ≠ 1; GRH, the generalised Riemann Hypothesis for these characters, is GRH_chi for every such χ; and the family theorem (family_theorem, v0.21) is compiled for a finite family at modulus q = 3.

WHAT THE KERNELS ASSUME. The programme's terminal table (relay data/terminal_table.json, 2016 rows) grades each compiled declaration by reading its statement: DERIVES, "the theorem's statement, unfolded to its base objects, establishes the claim"; INTERFACES, "the theorem takes the claim as a named hypothesis, discharged elsewhere and identified in the row. This is a legitimate architecture, not a defect; it is how a kernel cites a manuscript result. The row names the premise.". A premise is a binder asserting a Prop about a fixed object the kernel names and does not derive: a structure of premises, an EF_lit_* hypothesis, a Fact instance, a Summable or HasSum of the kernel's own series. By provenance, where a row's grade comes from, the rows are 209 cell, 1707 rule, 67 rule-elab and 1 none, with 32 rows upstream, declarations outside every kernel, apart. The keystone census at v0.6 counts the named premises the INTERFACES rows rest on and states its own sum: "50 heads carry rule-graded rows, their rows summing to 123, a row counted once for each named premise it rests on; the rule-elab rows beside sum to 47; 12 rows rest on a premise no head names. The table's INTERFACES rows, the upstream rows excluded, number 186: rule 117, rule-elab 46, cell 23." Read at their pins with five statuses computed from the kernels and not assigned by hand (relay data/b640_premise_status.txt), the kernels' INTERFACES rows rest on 25 open premises of the programme's own, each with its work-order on OPEN_TRAILS (25 of 25) -- BoundPremises, ConservationHypothesis, Dealigned, DealignedAt, EpsteinPremises, EulerFactorPremise, IdempotentAdd, IsEvenFn, IsExpansion, IsSignedCompletion, IsTrivialPoint, KeiperObligations, NontrivialZeroExistsInStrip, NotDiv, PerClassExcludes, Proper, Register4_channelInequality, Register4_positivity, SelfDualFE, TrivialSummandPremise, WindowObligations, ZeroActingPairing, farSmall, identity, is_xi_zero; 2 cited theorems -- NymanBeurlingPremise, PlattTrudgianHeight; 9 hypotheses of internal lemmas discharged where they are used -- Alternates, IsSign, PWSetup, StepsI, StepsMI, StructuralExhaustiveness, SymPairBound, ZetaSeam, zeroSideNeg; 2 premises shown non-vacuous by a witness and not discharged -- HCount, SatisfiesCWeil; and 12 domain conditions of general lemmas, which are not assumptions and are listed apart: Mathlib's predicates restricting a variable the statement quantifies -- AnalyticOnNhd (restricting R, f, g, s₀, w, z); Continuous (restricting f, j); EqOn (restricting Φ₁, Φ₂); HasCompactSupport (restricting f, j); HasDerivAt (restricting k, k', k'', x); Integrable (restricting φ); IsOpen (restricting H); IsRoot (restricting r); Monotone (restricting U); Prime (restricting p); StrictMono (restricting V); Tendsto (restricting φ).
