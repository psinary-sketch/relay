### (1) THE WRITEUP'S MAIN THEOREM, QUOTED VERBATIM FROM ITS OWN PAGE 1.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  | Theorem 1.1. For every nu > 0 there exist a force f in C^inf_c(R3 x (0, inf); R3), a compact set
  | K subset R3, and smooth velocity and pressure fields u, p on R3 x [0, 1) satisfying
  |     d_t u + (u . grad)u - nu Laplacian u + grad p = f,   div u = 0,   u(., 0) = 0,        (1.1)
  | such that supp u(., t) union supp p(., t) subset K for every 0 <= t < 1,
  |     sup_{0 <= t < 1} ||u(t)||_{L2(R3)} < infinity,
  |     limsup_{t -> 1-} ||u(t)||_{Linf(R3)} = infinity.
  | Consequently, there is no smooth solution (u, P) on R3 x [0, infinity) with the same force and
  | initial datum whose kinetic energy is uniformly bounded sup_{t >= 0} (1/2) INT_{R3} |u(x,t)|^2 dx
  | < infinity.
  | This establishes alternative (C) in the Millennium problem statement for Navier-Stokes as stated
  | by Fefferman in [13]. Compact support also yields the corresponding construction on T3 = R3/Z3,
  | establishing alternative (D) in [13]; see Corollary 10.6.

  ### ### **THEOREM 1.1 HAS TWO HALVES, AND THE SENTENCE `Consequently` IS THE HINGE:**
  ###   ### **(a) THE CONSTRUCTION** -- a force, a compact set and smooth fields on `[0, 1)` solving
  ###       the equations from rest, supported in `K`, of uniformly bounded `L2` norm, whose `Linf`
  ###       norm blows up as `t` rises to one.
  ###   ### **(b) THE CONSEQUENCE** -- no smooth global solution of uniformly bounded kinetic energy
  ###       for that same force and datum.
  ### ### **(b) IS CLAY'S ALTERNATIVE (C). ### (a) IS STRICTLY MORE THAN CLAY ASKS FOR**, because C
  ### requires only that no solution exist and never asks anyone to exhibit the blowup.
