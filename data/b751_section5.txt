**The classical theorem.** Z_Q0 meets the hypotheses of Voronin's theorem as Lee states it (Y. Lee, *On the Zeros of Epstein Zeta Functions*, arXiv:1204.6297v1, Theorem 1.1). D = −23 is negative. The class number of ℚ(√−23) is 3, above 1 (SIDEExplicitFormula/Schema/EpsteinZQ.lean :8). Q0 has integer coefficients, and its discriminant −23 is the field's. The theorem, due to S. M. Voronin (Trudy Mat. Inst. Steklov. 142 (1976), 135–147) and read in Lee's statement, says the following. For any σ1 and σ2 with 1/2 < σ1 < σ2 < 1 and for T sufficiently large, the region σ1 < Re s < σ2, |Im s| < T contains at least cT zeros of E(s, Q), where c = c(σ1, σ2, Q) > 0 does not depend on T. Lee's Theorem 1.3 is the later asymptotic form: cT + o(T) zeros in any such region with 1/2 < σ1 < σ2, and c > 0 if σ1 ≤ 1. Davenport and Heilbronn (J. London Math. Soc. 11 (1936), 181–185, 307–312), as Lee states their result, showed that when the class number exceeds 1, E(s, Q) has infinitely many zeros in Re s > 1. The sources read, and those not read, are listed in relay data/b751_literature.txt.

The located zero stands against these two theorems as follows. Its numerical value, with real part 0.9533, lies in the region of Voronin's theorem and outside the half-plane of Davenport and Heilbronn. Numerically, then, it is one instance of the phenomenon Voronin's theorem asserts, and not an instance of Davenport and Heilbronn's statement, which concerns Re s > 1. The certified enclosure does not carry that reading. 0.85326 ≤ Re ρ ≤ 1.05326 contains Re s = 1, so the Lean statement neither places the zero strictly between the line and Re s = 1 nor excludes Re ρ > 1. What Lean proves is Re ρ ≠ 1/2. The kernel's docstring cites Davenport and Heilbronn for another purpose: to widen the carrier past the closed strip, because an Epstein zeta of class number above 1 has infinitely many zeros with Re s > 1 (Schema/EpsteinZQ.lean :18-:20). That is the correct use of their theorem.
