### (C) COMPONENT 2 -- THE UNIT, FIXED HERE, BEFORE ANY SCORE.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
### ### **THE RUBRIC, QUOTED, IS WHAT THE PREDICATE IS BUILT FROM:** ### `KEYSTONES synthesize a
### cluster AGAINST other available content`. ### The order reads that as ### **COMBINES**, and gives
### the structural signature: ### **SOURCES FROM DIFFERENT CLUSTERS OCCUPY THE SAME UNIT.**

### ### ### **THE UNIT, DEFINED HERE AND NOT IN THE TOOL:** ### a `UNIT` is the maximal span of text
### that is exactly one of --
###   ### **(i) ONE LIST ITEM** ### -- a line opening with a bullet or an ordered marker, together with
###     its indented continuation lines;
###   ### **(ii) ONE TABLE ROW** ### -- a single line opening with a pipe;
###   ### **(iii) ONE PARAGRAPH** ### -- a run of consecutive non-blank lines that are neither of the
###     above, bounded by a blank line, by a real heading, or by a fence.
### ### **A REAL HEADING IS `b377`'S TEST**, not the corpus's `### **EMPHASIS**` convention, and it
### ### **CLOSES A UNIT AND IS NOT PART OF ONE.**
### ### ### **WHY A LIST ITEM AND A TABLE ROW ARE UNITS OF THEIR OWN, SAID BEFORE THE RUN:** ### under a
### blank-line-only definition a whole bibliography is ONE paragraph and ### **THE MOST PARTITIONED
### ### DOCUMENT IN THE CORPUS WOULD SCORE AS THE MOST COMBINING.** ### The order's own sentence --
### ### **`A COLLECTION PARTITIONS ITS SOURCES ONE PER ENTRY`** ### -- names the entry as the unit of
### partition, so an entry must be a unit or the predicate measures the opposite of what it is for.

### ### **A SOURCE IS `b380`'S, UNCHANGED AND IMPORTED:** ### a kernel named, or a corpus document
### named and resolved through the index, taken as its cluster. ### **SO REACH AND CO-LOCATION ARE
### ### MEASURED OVER THE SAME VOCABULARY** ### and the two columns are commensurable.
### ### **A UNIT CO-LOCATES** ### when it carries sources from ### **TWO OR MORE DISTINCT CLUSTERS.**
### Per document: ### `source-bearing units` ### (at least one source) and ### `co-locating units`,
### and the ratio of the second to the first.

### ### ### **THE THRESHOLD, DECLARED HERE AND NOT MOVED AFTERWARDS:**
###   ### **`C+`** ### -- more than half of its source-bearing units co-locate.
###   ### **`C-`** ### -- some do, but not more than half.
###   ### **`C?`** ### -- no unit carries a source at all.
### ### **THE REASON IS THE ORDER'S OWN WORDING:** ### `partitions` and `co-locates` are claims about
### ### **HOW A DOCUMENT IS BUILT** -- about most of it -- and not about whether one line happens to
### name two things. ### **A SINGLE-OCCURRENCE RULE WOULD MAKE A BIBLIOGRAPHY WITH ONE `see also`
### ### ENTRY A SYNTHESIS.**
### ### ### **AND IF THE CONTROL DISAGREES THE THRESHOLD IS NOT MOVED TO MAKE IT AGREE**, which is the
### clause `b380` paid for and this act inherits. ### **THE FULL RATIO PER EXEMPLAR IS PRINTED**, so a
### reader can see what any other threshold would have done without this seat trying one.

### ### **WHAT THE PREDICATE IS DEAF TO, DECLARED HERE AND NOT DISCOVERED LATER:**
###   ### **A DOCUMENT QUOTING MANY SOURCES IN ONE PLACE WITHOUT RELATING THEM SCORES AS SYNTHESIS.**
###     ### The order names this one and asks whether the exemplars test it; ### **WHETHER THEY DO IS
###     ### REPORTED AS A MEASUREMENT AND NOT AS AN ASSURANCE.**
###   ### **A SYNTHESIS THAT RELATES TWO SOURCES ACROSS ADJACENT PARAGRAPHS SCORES AS GATHERING.**
###     ### Co-location cannot see across a paragraph break, and a writer's habit is not an argument.
###   ### **MARKDOWN STRUCTURE STANDS IN FOR RHETORICAL STRUCTURE.** ### A document written as a few
###     long paragraphs co-locates by formatting; one written in short ones cannot.
###   ### **THE DIRECTORY STANDS IN FOR THE SUBJECT** -- `b380`'s, inherited whole, an ADDRESS standing
###     in for a subject and exactly the substitution `(R2)` warns against.
###   ### **A CITATION IT DOES NOT RECOGNISE IS NOT A CITATION TO IT** -- `b380`'s, inherited whole.
