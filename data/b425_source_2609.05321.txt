ABSTRACT
Current consensus suggests that Type Ia supernova (SN Ia) brightnesses post light-curve standard-
ization correlate with their host-galaxy stellar masses, which must be accounted for to obtain accurate
cosmological constraints. For example, M. Vincenzi et al. (2025) showed that different host-galaxy
stellar mass measurements for the same dataset produce redshift-dependent differences of order∼0.01
mag, large enough to appreciably shift cosmological constraints. We present internally consistent host-
galaxy stellar masses remeasured using aperture photometry and spectral energy distribution (SED)
fitting for SN-Unite, which combines the spectroscopic Pantheon+ and the photometric Dark Energy
Survey five-year (DES-SN5YR) samples into the largest SN Ia cosmology sample to date, with 2884
likely SNe Ia. We find that photometry and SED fitting choices shift SN-Unite FlatwCDM parameters
well below statistical uncertainties. Our stellar masses differ from the Pantheon+ data release partly
due to a redshift-dependent internal inconsistency within Pantheon+, while remaining largely consis-

1.INTRODUCTION
Type Ia Supernovae (SNe Ia) are powerful cosmologi-
cal probes, especially in the context of dark energy. Not
only did SNe Ia observations provide conclusive evidence
of the accelerated expansion of the universe (A. G. Riess
et al. 1998; S. Perlmutter et al. 1999), more than 25
years of observations (e.g., P. Astier et al. 2006; W. M.
Wood-Vasey et al. 2007a; R. Kessler et al. 2009a; M.
Sullivan et al. 2011; M. Betoule et al. 2014; D. Brout
et al. 2022; DES Collaboration et al. 2024; D. Rubin
et al. 2025) have significantly tightened the constraints
on the dark energy equation of state,w=P/ρ. The
preference for an accelerated expansion based on mea-
surements of the deceleration parameterq 0 has accord-
ingly increased from around 3σin the late 1990s to
over 5σwith the recent Dark Energy Survey five-year
analysis (DES-SN5YR, DES Collaboration et al. 2024).
That trend continues with this current work, which is
a companion paper to the SN-Unite cosmology paper
that combines Pantheon+ and DES-SN5YR SNe Ia (R.
Camilleri et al. in prep. 2026).
Furthermore, SNe Ia are independent probes of
dark energy, providing complementary information to
other probes of dark energy: the Cosmic Microwave
Background (CMB) and Baryon Acoustic Oscillations
(BAO). Therefore, combining all three measurements
significantly tightens constraints on cosmological pa-
rameters. While DES-SN5YR (DES Collaboration et al.
2024) reports (Ω m, w) = (0.264 +0.074
−0.096,−0.80 +0.14
−0.16) for
SNe Ia alone constraints in FlatwCDM, the Dark En-
ergy Spectroscopic Instrument Data Release 2 (DESI
DR2, M. Abdul Karim et al. 2025) reports (Ω m, w) =
(0.3098±0.0050,−0.971±0.021) when DES-SN5YR is
combined with DESI BAO and CMB fromPlanck

M. S. Madhavacheril et al. 2024; F. J. Qu et al. 2024),
and the South Pole Telescope (SPT) (J. Carlstrom et al.
2011).

The use of SNe Ia as standardizable candles relies on
the assumption that SNe Ia are consistent in their peak
brightnesses, and that their intrinsic differences can be
accounted for using empirical techniques. One common
method to standardize SNe Ia for cosmology is to fit
for their light-curves using light-curve fitters such as
the Spectral Adaptive Light Curve Template (SALT)
(SALT1 & SALT2, J. Guy et al. 2005, 2007), (SALT3,
W. Kenworthy et al. 2021) to determine the light-curve
parametersx 1 (‘stretch’) andc(‘color’) for each SN
Ia used for cosmological analysis. After the stretch and
color corrections, a residual correlation between SN Ia
luminosities and host-galaxy properties remains. In Fig-
ure 1, we show the correlation between the Hubble resid-
uals and the host-galaxy stellar mass, when the host-
galaxy stellar mass step is not accounted for (setting

The correlation was originally discovered as a de-
pendence on host-galaxy stellar mass, with SNe Ia in
higher stellar mass galaxies found to be 0.05 to 0.10 mag
brighter than those in lower stellar mass galaxies after
correcting for stretch and color (P. L. Kelly et al. 2010;
H. Lampeitl et al. 2010; M. Sullivan et al. 2010) (>4σ
level for the latter two). M. Sullivan et al. (2010) further
found that including a host-galaxy stellar ‘mass step’ im-
proves theχ 2 fit to the data, and that not accounting
for the mass step can cause shifts inwcomparable to
its statistical uncertainty in FlatwCDM.
Subsequent analyses found that alternative global
host-galaxy properties show similar dependence - rest-
frame color, emission lines, metallicity, age, star forma-
tion rate, as well as dust (e.g., C. B. D’Andrea et al.
2011; R. R. Gupta et al. 2011; M. Moreno-Raya et al.
2018; M. Roman et al. 2018; L. Galbany et al. 2022;
J. Duarte et al. 2023; L. Kelsey et al. 2023; C. Meldorf
et al. 2023; P. Wiseman et al. 2023; B. Martin et al. 2024;
M. Dixon et al. 2025). Several works have additionally
sought to characterize the correlation of SN Ia luminosi-
ties with local properties in the vicinity of the SNe Ia
(M. Rigault et al. 2013; M. Roman et al. 2018; M. Gino-
lin et al. 2025) due to concerns that global host-galaxy
properties are not representative of the SN Ia environ-
ments.
While some of the aforementioned alternative ap-
proaches are more physically motivated, there is not
yet a clear consensus that such approaches provide a
more robust correction than the global host-galaxy stel-
lar mass. Moreover, global host-galaxy stellar masses are
generally more straightforward to measure accurately
and precisely across large, heterogeneous SN Ia sam-
ples. As such, host-galaxy stellar masses have continued
to be used for SN Ia cosmological analyses to account
for host-dependence of SNe Ia luminosities, including in
three of the most widely utilized recent SN Ia analyses:
Pantheon+ (D. Brout et al. 2022), DES-SN5YR (DES
Collaboration et al. 2024), and Union3 (D. Rubin et al.
2025). Therefore, we limit our scope to host-galaxy stel-
lar masses rather than other host-related parameters in
this work.

While recent surveys such as DES-SN5YR and Pan-
theon+, as well as earlier surveys like the Supernova
Legacy Survey (SNLS) and the Joint Light-curve Anal-
ysis (JLA) (A. Conley et al. 2010; M. Sullivan et al. 2011;
M. Betoule et al. 2014), used a ‘mass step’ to account
for the host-galaxy mass, other analyses have considered
smooth functions and the redshift evolution of the ‘mass
step’ (M. Vincenzi et al. 2024; B. Popovic et al. 2025;
D. Rubin et al. 2025, 2026).
M. Smith et al. (2020a) additionally explores the sys-
tematic uncertainties of their host-galaxy stellar mass
estimates for the 206 spectroscopically confirmed Dark
Energy Survey ‘three-year’ (DES-SN3YR, T. Abbott
et al. 2019) analysis. They find that their host mass
estimates are robust to systematic variants such as in-
troducing additional bursts in star-formation history or
using a different initial mass function (IMF), meaning
that such variants can result in an overall systematic
shift by 0.1 to 0.5 dex, but not a stellar mass-dependent
offset.
Since the report of the preference of time-evolving
dark energy over FlatΛCDM (DES Collaboration et al.
2024; A. Adame et al. 2025; M. Abdul Karim et al.
2025) with>3σsignificance when the DESI DR1/DR2
BAO are combined with the CMB and the DES-
SN5YR or Union3 datasets, SN Ia cosmology has been
under increased scrutiny (G. Efstathiou 2025; A. No-
tari et al. 2025). This is partly because the best-
fit Flatw 0waCDM results under the linear Chevallier-
Polarski-Linder (CPL) parametrization (wherew=
w0 +w a(1−a), M. Chevallier & D. Polarski 2001; E. V.
Linder 2003) are especially sensitive to low-z(z <0.2)
SN Ia data where other cosmological constraints are
largely absent.
G. Efstathiou (2025) in particular, raised the possibil-
ity that the increased preference for time-evolving dark
energy when DES-SN5YR (3.9σin DESI DR1 based on
the maximuma posteriori(MAP)) is used instead of
Pantheon+ (2.5σin DESI DR1 based on the MAP) to
combine with DESI DR1 BAO and the CMB, was due
to unaddressed systematics in the DES-SN5YR analy-
sis, leading to a∼0.04 mag difference between the low-
zand high-zoverlapping samples of DES-SN5YR and
Pantheon+. In a response to G. Efstathiou (2025), M.
Vincenzi et al. (2025) found that these differences are
justified in part because the photometrically classified
DES-SN5YR sample is substantially more complete and

More recently, the DES-Dovekie analysis (B. Popovic

While the T. J. Hoyt et al. (2026) reanalysis of Pan-
theon+ with updated host-galaxy stellar masses was
not done with the same analysis framework as Pan-
theon+ (D. Brout et al. 2022; D. Scolnic et al. 2022),
the concordance between the three currently most often
utilized SN Ia datasets, in terms of the preference for
time-evolving dark energy when DESI-DR2 BAO and
PlanckCMB are combined, is striking.
For some context, Pantheon+ and Union3/Union3.1
share a similar dataset, using a compilation of histori-
cal samples of spectroscopically confirmed SNe Ia, while
DES-SN5YR and DES-Dovekie mostly use photometri-
cally classified SNe Ia from the DES-SN program (A.
M¨ oller & T. de Boissi` ere 2020; H. Qu et al. 2021; A.
M¨ oller et al. 2022; M. Vincenzi et al. 2023; B. S´ anchez
et al. 2024), with a small (∼200) external low-zsam-
ple that is largely a subset of the Pantheon+ and Union
low-zdatasets. All three datasets have a similar number
of SNe Ia (around 1500 to 2000) and therefore simi-
lar statistical constraining powers on cosmological pa-
rameters. For the methodology, Pantheon+ and DES-
SN5YR (DES-Dovekie) use the same framework, which
uses Beams with Bias Corrections (BBC, R. Kessler
& D. Scolnic 2017) to perform simulations-based cor-
rections to model selection effects, while Union uses
the Unified Nonlinear Inference for Type Ia cosmologY
(UNITY, D. Rubin et al. 2015, 2025), a Bayesian hi-
erarchical model that simultaneously models the SN Ia
light-curve parameters, selection effects and the cosmo-
logical parameters.
In the light of such tantalizing developments, SN-
Unite (R. Camilleri et al. in prep. 2026) combines the
DES-SN5YR and Pantheon+ datasets to create the
largest SN Ia dataset to date numbering 2884 likely SNe
Ia (P Ia >0.8, with about 46% of SNe Ia spectroscop-
ically confirmed), and provides the tightest constraints
to date on Ωm and dark energy using SNe Ia only, as well
as with combinations of SNe Ia, BAO, and the CMB.
Due to the developments elucidated above, it is cru-
cial that we investigate host-galaxy mass measurements
and their impact on cosmology in detail. For this rea-
son, we remeasure the host-galaxy stellar masses for the
SN-Unite sample with a single, consistent pipeline, and
assess how measurement choices impact the SN-Unite as
well as the cosmological analyses of the Pantheon+ and
DES-SN5YR subsamples within SN-Unite.
This work is organized as follows. In Section 2, we
describe our dataset and in Section 3, we describe our
methodology, including our photometry pipeline, as well
as the spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting we use
to estimate the host-galaxy stellar mass. In Section 4,
we show our host-galaxy stellar mass measurements,
and compare with previous measurements in the liter-
ature. We describe and assess the impact of different
methodology choices on cosmology in Section 5.1. In
Section 5.2, we show cosmological constraints for the
DES-SN5YR and Pantheon+ subsamples within SN-
Unite and assess the impact of updating the data-release
masses with SN-Unite masses, in FlatwCDM using SNe
Ia only, and in Flatw 0waCDM using SNe Ia + BAO
+ CMB. We end with a Discussion and Conclusion in
Section 6.

2.2.Photometry Dataset
Although high-quality host-galaxy spectra can provide
more precise and accurate host-galaxy properties, spec-
tra are not uniformly available for the SN-Unite host-
galaxies. In addition, the spectra are often not of high
enough quality for spectral fitting since the primary pur-
pose of taking spectra (at least for the case of DES) was
to obtain host redshifts. Hence the first step in measur-
ing host-galaxy properties is to obtain photometry of
the host-galaxies. In this work, we use optical and near-
ultraviolet observations for our default host-galaxy stel-
lar masses. For the optical wavelengths, we use the DES
grizbands from 5-year median coadded images as in H.
Qu et al. (2024) for the DES-SN5YR hosts. While P.
Wiseman et al. (2020) (W20) compiled DESgrizhost-
galaxy photometry using 4-year (excluding the season
of SN Ia explosion) coadded images for DES-SN5YR,
we re-derive the photometry for SN-Unite to maintain
a consistent photometry pipeline throughout all of the
host-galaxy sample. We note that median coadded im-

We additionally considered the risk of SNe Ia light

the three surveys were observed during different years.

used, we find that adding2MASS JHKsphotometry
degrades the quality of the spectral energy distribution
(SED) fit described in Section 3.2 for the majority of our
host-galaxies, likely due to the near-IR image resolution
being suboptimal for our analysis, with theJHKspho-
tometry scatter being around 0.8 mag. Nonetheless, we
include the2MASS JHKsphotometry when considering
variants of our mass measurements in Sections 4 and 5.
We find that the addition of the near-UV photometry
reduces the median host-galaxy stellar mass uncertainty
by about 10% (from 0.25 dex to 0.23 dex, see left plot
of Figure 10 in Appendix A), but does not change the
median host-galaxy log stellar mass measurements, as
shown in Table 5.
We note that our wavelength coverage of 180−1000
nm atz= 0 does not probe the same parts of the
galaxy spectra at higher redshifts. Atz >0.5, we are
no longer sensitive to any part of the rest-frame near-
IR, and atz >1, we are only sensitive to rest-frame UV
and blue/green optical wavelengths. A. Paulino-Afonso
et al. (2022) find that inconsistent rest-frame coverage
in photometry can cause up to a∼0.3 dex underesti-
mation of host-galaxy stellar masses atz∼1, but shifts
cosmological constraints by less than 1%. Additionally,
we find that not including the near-IR (or near-UV) has
a small impact on our mass measurements (see Table 5
and Figure 5 for more details), although other host prop-
erties are more sensitive to the wavelength coverage.

3.METHODOLOGY
We describe our host-galaxy stellar mass estimation
pipeline in this section.
3.1.Photometry
As mentioned in Section 2.2, we start with photome-
try of the host-galaxies. We use theHostPhot(v3.1.3) 31
(T. E. M¨ uller-Bravo & L. Galbany 2022) package to
perform aperture photometry using host RA and Dec
coordinates obtained by DES-SN5YR, SNLS, PS1MD,
SDSS, and Pantheon+, largely based on the directional-
light-radius (DLR) approach to identify host-galaxies
(M. Sullivan et al. 2006; R. R. Gupta et al. 2016). While
model-fitting (e.g., fitting a Sersic profile) as in H. Qu
et al. (2024) is also possible, we perform aperture pho-
tometry as done in Pantheon+ and DES (P. Wiseman
et al. 2020) in order to keep the photometry pipeline
consistent throughout all redshifts.
Host-galaxy photometry withHostPhotbegins with
downloading image cutouts; we use 3 ′ by 3′ cutouts for
zhelio <0.03 hosts (larger for the nearest galaxies), 1.5 ′

by 0.5 ′ cutouts forz helio ≥0.10 hosts wherez helio de-

dian coadds, we use a custom code to obtain 100 pixel by
100 pixel (roughly 0.45′ by 0.45′) cutouts. For reference,
3′ corresponds to roughly 108 kpc atz= 0.03, while
1.5′ corresponds to roughly 166 kpc atz= 0.1, and
0.5′ corresponds to roughly 55 kpc atz= 0.1 assum-
ing a FlatΛCDM cosmology withH 0 = 70km/s/Mpc
and Ω m = 0.3. We useriz(grzwheniis not avail-

Trueand set the defaultepsto 0.001, which means that
the Kron radius is increased until the change in flux is
less than 0.1%. We also use the masking functionality to
mask contaminants. We keep the aperture and masking

user-input model parameters. We use thesfhdelayed,
m2005, and thedustatt_modified_starburstmod-

(SFH) is modeled as:

While H. Qu et al. (2024) use thebc03library of
single stellar populations (SSPs) with a Salpeter initial
mass function (IMF) (E. E. Salpeter 1955), we use the
m2005(C. Maraston 2005) SSP in order to assume a
Kroupa IMF (P. Kroupa 2001) for our default masses.
(CIGALE does not allow the user to assume a Kroupa
IMF withbc03.) M. Smith et al. (2020a) and P. Wise-
man et al. (2020) also assume a Kroupa IMF but use
underlying spectral synthesis templates fromP ´EGASE.2
(M. Fioc & B. Rocca-Volmerange 1997; D. Le Borgne
& B. Rocca-Volmerange 2002) when determining the
DES-SN5YR host-galaxy masses. The main SFH param-
eter grids span:tau_main= 1 to 8000 Myr,age_main
= 1000 to 10000 Myr, andf_burst= 0.001 to 0.60,
with themetallicity= 0.02 andseparation_age=
10 Myr. The default CIGALE input parameters used
in this analysis will be included in the SN-Unite data-
release. With this configuration, one CIGALE run using
128 cores takes about 2 minutes for 1,000 hosts. While
it is possible to define a finer grid of parameters, in-
creasing the total grid size by a factor of 50 leads to
only a small change in the SED fitting results while in-
creasing the runtime to∼1 hour. Given our relatively
restricted wavelength coverage, the photometry limits
our ability to robustly constrain complex star-formation
histories. We therefore checked that settingf_burst=
0 does not affect the host-galaxy stellar mass measure-
ments significantly enough for cosmological constraints
to be altered more than for the variants considered in
Table 4.
Additionally, we compare each of the multi-band host-
galaxy fluxes from the CIGALE SED fit with the input
flux fromHostPhotto identifyindividualbands with
issues. For<3% of the host-galaxies, the image cutouts
for one or two specific band(s) have issues such as bad
pixels, in which case we remove the specific band(s) and
rerun the SED fitting.
3.2.1.Host-Galaxy Redshifts
CIGALE requires either a redshift or an explicit
distance to set the luminosity distance, which deter-
mines the conversion between intrinsic model luminosi-
ties and the observed fluxes used in the SED fit. For the
CIGALE redshift (or distance) inputs, we mostly use
host-galaxy redshifts from the DES-SN5YR and Pan-
theon+ data-release, which are all spectroscopic red-
shifts. Forz HD ≥0.01 hosts, we use the Hubble Dia-
gram redshift, which includes peculiar-velocity correc-
tions. For hosts atz HD <0.01, we instead use the
CMB frame redshift as peculiar-velocity corrections be-
come increasingly uncertain at very low redshifts. At
zCMB <0.003, peculiar velocities become comparable
to the Hubble expansion velocity, meaning that redshift-
based distances are no longer reliable. Therefore, for the
6 unique hosts wherez CMB <0.003 (NGC 4424, M101,
M82, NGC 5253, NGC 4526, and NGC 2841), we use
direct distance measurements in Mpc from S. Sakai &
B. F. Madore (1999); L. M. Macri et al. (2001); D. Hatt
et al. (2018); E. Sabbi et al. (2018); R. L. Beaton et al.
(2019).

5.IMPACT ON COSMOLOGY
We assess the impact of host-galaxy stellar mass mea-
surements on cosmology in two cases: (i) replacing the

5.3.Importance of Consistent Host-Galaxy Stellar
Mass Measurements
The analysis in Sections 5.1 and 5.2 can be sum-
marized as follows. While the specific host-galaxy stel-
lar mass measurement choices have minimal impact on
the SN-Unite cosmological constraints, it is crucial to
maintain a consistent measurement pipeline across all
of the SN Ia sample considered for cosmology. This was
true for the DES-SN5YR/DES-Dovekie and Union3.1
samples, while combining various different host-galaxy
mass measurements with different underlying assump-
tions has introduced systematic offsets in both the of-
ficial Pantheon+ mass measurements and their cosmo-
logical constraints.

This analysis is not the end of investigations of host-
galaxy correlations with SN Ia brightnesses post light-
curve corrections. As briefly outlined in Section 1, future
work should strive to find a more physically motivated,
yet reliable method to account for host-galaxy correla-
tions with SN Ia luminosities, as opposed to using the
global host-galaxy stellar mass. One promising pathway
may be the two-stretch population approach shown in
D. Rubin et al. (2026). In addition, future work should
fit for the host-galaxy mass-step location, rather than
fixing it to 10 10M⊙, as done in this work.
Nonetheless, the analysis provided in R. Camilleri
et al. (in prep. 2026), as well as this current work, shows
that the 3 representative SN Ia cosmological constraints
(Union3.1, DES-Dovekie, and Pantheon+) agree with
each other, and combining each of them with BAO and
CMB points to a∼3σpreference of time-evolving dark
energy over FlatΛCDM under a frequentist interpre-
tation. By re-measuring the host-galaxy stellar masses
consistently throughout all of SN-Unite, we mitigate po-
tential systematic shifts in the cosmological constraints
associated with heterogeneous host-galaxy stellar mass
measurements, while providing the tightest cosmological
constraints to date.
