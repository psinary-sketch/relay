ANSWER 1:
The claim is a reduction, not a proof. In the README's words: "RH reduced to a single located clause, reduction machine-verified." RH is the Riemann Hypothesis as Mathlib states it. The single clause is h2_sign, which is Weil positivity: for every admissible test window k in classK, the pole term minus the prime sum plus the archimedean term of the explicit formula, P(k) − PR(k) + A(k), is at least zero. The Lean theorem h2_sign_iff_rh is said to prove, with no premise and using only the standard three axioms, that h2_sign is equivalent to RH. The path from h2_sign to RH goes through the seam rh_strip_imp_rh, which the kernel also proves (rh_strip_imp_rh_holds). The document says that none of the many other OPEN premises in its table lie on this path; they are assumed only by theorems that this reduction does not use. It also lists other machine-checked theorems, each graded DERIVES or INTERFACES.

ANSWER 2:
The document says plainly that it does not claim "RH proved". h2_sign is open and is carried as the one open premise. Nothing in the record states that the Riemann Hypothesis holds. Several related statements are also left open:
- the exhaustiveness clause covers_all (every zero is produced by one of the seven named classes)
- the "squeeze" target (a uniform lower bound on |ξ′(ρ)| at the zeros)
The document calls both of these faces of the same open node, equivalent to RH "given the surround". The document also does not claim to discharge any of the other OPEN premises, such as EpsteinPremises or BoundPremises. CITED premises such as NymanBeurlingPremise and PlattTrudgianHeight are taken from the literature, not compiled. WITNESSED premises have only been shown satisfiable, not proved for the objects the theorems actually use.

ANSWER 3:
The refuted premise is TrivialSummandPremise, and its status is REFUTED-BY-COMPUTATION. It required a pole term to be zero, but that term is positive at an object the programme exhibits. So the premise as stated is false, and no witness of it can exist. I read not_trivialSummandPremise (DERIVES) as the theorem that records this, but the document does not say so outright. It was replaced by TrivialSummandPremise', a restated premise that carries the pole term in place of the zero. The kernel witnesses it at the constant 1 (trivialSummandPremise'_witness). The theorem dedekind_rhs' is proved on this restated premise together with EulerFactorPremise, whose status is WITNESSED.
