ANSWER 1:
The claim, in the README's words, is "RH reduced to a single located clause, reduction machine-verified." Lean 4 kernels prove, with no premise (theorem h2_sign_iff_rh, graded DERIVES, using only the standard three axioms), that the Riemann Hypothesis as Mathlib states it is equivalent to one sign statement, h2_sign. h2_sign is Weil positivity: for every admissible test window k in classK, the explicit-formula quadratic form P(k) − PR(k) + A(k) (pole term minus prime sum plus archimedean term) is non-negative. The document says the path from h2_sign to RH goes through a seam, rh_strip_imp_rh, which the kernel proves (rh_strip_imp_rh_holds), and that no OPEN premise lies on that path. It also lists other machine-checked theorems, gives each premise a status, and reports that the SIDE-kernel route terminals exclude seven named mechanism classes as sources of an off-line zero.

ANSWER 2:
It does not claim that RH is proved. The document says plainly that "RH proved" is a sentence the corpus does not support, that h2_sign is still open and carried as the one open premise, and that nothing in the record says RH holds. The mechanism exclusions do not prove h2_sign. The exhaustiveness of the seven mechanism classes over all mechanisms is not compiled. Several premises remain OPEN or only CITED (for example NymanBeurlingPremise and PlattTrudgianHeight are cited from the literature, not compiled). The "squeeze" target, a uniform lower bound on |ξ′(ρ)| at the zeros, is described as the same open node seen geometrically, not as something proved or as a second open premise.

ANSWER 3:
The refuted premise is TrivialSummandPremise, which has the status REFUTED-BY-COMPUTATION. As stated it requires a pole term to be zero, but that pole term is positive at an object the programme exhibits, so the premise is false and nothing can witness it. It was replaced by TrivialSummandPremise', which carries the pole term. The theorem dedekind_rhs' is proved on this restated premise. A theorem not_trivialSummandPremise (DERIVES) is also listed, and presumably records the refutation, though the text does not say that outright.
