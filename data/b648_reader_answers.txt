ANSWER 1:
Maturity is a column added in edition v0.8. The definition is in the v0.8 back matter ("The readings v0.8 adds"), not in the glossary. It says how far a document's claims are licensed by the kernels, judged from that document's rows in the licensed-statement table (relay tools/licensed_table.py). The rule takes four values, tested in this order:
- SUPERSEDED, if REGISTRY marks the keystone as superseded by a later edition;
- otherwise ONGOING, if any of its rows is graded OVERREACHES or UNLICENSED (claims the kernels do not yet license);
- otherwise CARRIED-OPENLY, if any row states a frontier as open (a claim stated as open, or a theorem at INTERFACES on a premise whose status is OPEN);
- otherwise SETTLED (definitions and compiled statements).
UNDERSTATES does not change the value, because it means the document says less than the corpus licenses. A document that no table reaches is left blank, marked "NOT YET IN THE TABLE", and is not counted as having a value. Maturity is therefore a reading of how far claims match the licensing table, not a judgement of quality. The rule is worded for "keystones", but the census applies it to Day 1 companions and kernels as well. The text does not define OVERREACHES, UNLICENSED or MATCHES beyond the brief gloss in parentheses.
