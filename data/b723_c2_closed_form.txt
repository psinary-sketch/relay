## THE SETTING
  K an imaginary quadratic field of discriminant D < -4 and class number h(D) = 2; Cl(K) = {C0, C1}, C0 the principal class; Q the principal
  form (x^2 + (-D/4) y^2 or x^2 + xy + ((1 - D)/4) y^2). Z_Q(s) = SUM' Q(x, y)^-s over (x, y) != (0, 0). The units are +-1, so
  Z_Q(s) = 2 zeta(s, C0), zeta(s, C) = SUM over integral ideals a in C of N(a)^-s. b(n) is the coefficient of n^-s in log(Z_Q / 2).
  A rational prime p is BAD when it splits or ramifies in K and its prime ideals lie in C1 (chi_D(p) != -1 and Q does not represent p);
  GOOD otherwise (inert, or split or ramified into principal prime ideals). e_p = 2 for a bad split p (two prime ideals of norm p), 1 for a
  bad ramified p (one). S(s) = SUM over bad p of e_p artanh(p^-s).

## THE STATEMENT
  log(Z_Q / 2) = log zeta_K - S + log cosh S.

## THE FOUR STEPS
  (1) Z_Q / 2 = (zeta_K + L(psi)) / 2, psi the genus character (psi(C0) = 1, psi(C1) = -1):
      zeta_K = zeta(s, C0) + zeta(s, C1) and L(s, psi) = zeta(s, C0) - zeta(s, C1); add and halve.
  (2) L(psi) / zeta_K = e^(-2S): both are Euler products over the prime ideals P, zeta_K = PROD (1 - N(P)^-s)^-1 and
      L(psi) = PROD (1 - psi(P) N(P)^-s)^-1. They differ only at the P with psi(P) = -1, the prime ideals in C1: an inert p gives
      P = (p), principal; a split or ramified p gives prime ideals all in one class (P and its conjugate are inverse, and C1 is its own
      inverse), so the P with psi(P) = -1 are exactly those above the bad primes, e_p of them, each of norm p. Hence
      L(psi) / zeta_K = PROD over bad p of ((1 - p^-s) / (1 + p^-s))^(e_p), and (1 - x) / (1 + x) = e^(-2 artanh x) gives e^(-2S).
  (3) (1 + e^(-2S)) / 2 = e^(-S) (e^S + e^(-S)) / 2 = e^(-S) cosh S.
  (4) The logarithm: Z_Q / 2 = zeta_K e^(-S) cosh S by (1)-(3), so log(Z_Q / 2) = log zeta_K - S + log cosh S, for Re s > 1, where each
      series converges absolutely.
