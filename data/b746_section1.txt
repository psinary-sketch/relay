### THE SECTION AS DRAFTED (written verbatim under :5 on a pass) BEGIN
The object of this paper is one Epstein zeta function, that of the binary quadratic form

  Q0(x, y) = x² + xy + 6y²,

of discriminant 1 − 24 = −23. It is defined in the kernel SIDE-explicit-formula as `Q0form` (SIDEExplicitFormula/Schema/EpsteinZQ.lean :45 at the tag v0.38, 14ca5725); `four_mul_Q0form` (:48) writes 4·Q0 = (2x + y)² + 23y², so the form is positive definite. Every citation of a Lean name below is to that tag, by path and line; every figure is to a relay bank line.

**The class group.** The reduced positive definite forms of discriminant −23 are Q0 and the pair

  Q1(x, y) = 2x² + xy + 3y²,  Q1⁻¹(x, y) = 2x² − xy + 3y²,

so the form class group is cyclic of order 3 (the class number the kernel's module states, EpsteinZQ.lean :8), with Q0 its identity and Q1, Q1⁻¹ the two classes of order 3. The substitution (x, y) ↦ (x, −y) carries Q1 to Q1⁻¹, so the two take the same values with the same multiplicities. The kernel at v0.38 defines Q0 alone; Q1, the class group and the two L-functions below carry no Lean definition, and enter here as relay data/b725_interference.txt reads them.

**The Epstein zeta.** For a form Q of discriminant −23 let r_Q(k) be the number of integer pairs (m, n) with Q(m, n) = k, the origin excluded. Then

  Z_Q(s) = Σ′_{(m,n) ≠ (0,0)} Q(m, n)^(−s) = Σ_{k ≥ 1} r_Q(k) k^(−s),  Re s > 1,

the lattice sum over nonzero pairs. For Q0 the kernel defines the coefficients as `rQ` (EpsteinZQ.lean :59-:62, with r_Q(1) = 2: the pairs (±1, 0)) and the sum as `ZQ`, Mathlib's `LSeries` of `rQ` (:69); `ZQ` is Z_Q0 where the series converges. Since Q1 and Q1⁻¹ have the same values, Z_{Q1⁻¹} = Z_{Q1}, and the three classes give two functions, Z_Q0 and Z_Q1.

**The completion.** The gamma factor's base is `qBase` = 2π/√23 (EpsteinZQ.lean :72), and the completed function is

  Λ_Q(s) = (√23/2π)^s Γ(s) Z_Q(s) = qBase^(−s) Γ(s) Z_Q(s),

the form the kernel's named premise `ZQContinuation` states its functional equation Λ_Q(s) = Λ_Q(1 − s) in (EpsteinZQ.lean :80, :84-:85). In the tree Λ_Q is built from the theta of the form rather than from the sum: `thetaU` u = Σ_{(m,n)} exp(−π u Q0(m, n)) (SIDEExplicitFormula/Schema/ZQ/Theta.lean :20), rescaled by `cc` = 2/√23 (ZQ/FEPair.lean :22) into `thetaC` (:37), the pair `zqPair` (:158), and `lamQ` := `zqPair`.Λ, its Mellin transform (ZQ/EF/Xi.lean :26). The half of that Mellin integral over t > 1, M(w) = ∫_{t>1} t^(w−1)(θ(t) − 1) dt, is `lamM` (ZQ/OffZero/Split.lean :29). The continuation of Z_Q0 to s ≠ 1 is `zqFn` (ZQ/Continuation.lean :122-:123), written from Λ₀ with the pole at 0 absorbed by Γ(s + 1)⁻¹. The statements tying these together — the Mellin transform equal to qBase^(−s) Γ(s) Z_Q on Re s > 1 (ZQ/Continuation.lean :76-:77), and the continuation's properties — are discharges, and are §3's.

**The zeros.** The zeros of Λ_Q, with their orders, form the configuration `zqZeroConfig` (ZQ/Zeros.lean :319), whose carrier is `LambdaQZeros` `zqFn` (:320; `LambdaQZeros`, EpsteinZQ.lean :92): the zeros of the continuation off 0, 1 and the trivial zeros −1, −2, …. The configuration is not confined to the critical strip. The kernel's reason, stated where Z_Q is defined, is that an Epstein zeta of class number above 1 has infinitely many zeros with Re s > 1 (Davenport–Heilbronn, as EpsteinZQ.lean :18-:20 cites it), so the carrier is of the widened class `WideConfig`.

**ζ_K and L(s, ψ).** Let K = ℚ(√−23), whose class group the three forms represent, and let ψ be a character of order 3 of that group, ψ(Q1) = ω = e^(2πi/3). With w = 2 units, the Dedekind zeta and the class-group L-function are sums of the Epstein zetas over the classes, divided by w:

  ζ_K(s) = (Z_Q0(s) + 2 Z_Q1(s))/2,
  L(s, ψ) = (Z_Q0(s) + (ω + ω²) Z_Q1(s))/2 = (Z_Q0(s) − Z_Q1(s))/2,

and ψ̄ gives the same L. On the coefficients the first identity reads r_Q0(n) + 2 r_Q1(n) = 2 Σ_{d|n} (−23/d), a classical identity (the count of ideals of norm n in K, times the two units), corroborated exactly for n ≤ 3000 (relay data/b725_interference.txt :8); the second gives the coefficients (r_Q0(n) − r_Q1(n))/2, Hecke’s, the q-expansion of η(z)η(23z), corroborated for n ≤ 3000 (:9). Solving the two identities for the principal form,

  Z_Q0(s) = (2/3)(ζ_K(s) + 2 L(s, ψ)).

**No Euler product.** ζ_K and L(s, ψ) have Euler products; Z_Q0 does not. Its normalised coefficients r_Q0(n)/2 are not multiplicative: r_Q0(2) = r_Q0(3) = 0 while r_Q0(6) = 4 (relay data/b725_interference.txt :11). Z_Q0 is a sum of two Euler products, not a product of them. Two things follow for what is built on it. Its zeros are neither the zeros of ζ_K nor those of L(s, ψ); at a zero ρ of Z_Q0 the identities force ζ_K(ρ) = Z_Q1(ρ) = −2 L(ρ, ψ) (the column of :31). And because the logarithmic derivative of a sum is not the sum of the logarithmic derivatives, the zero side of an explicit formula for Z_Q0 is not linear in the decomposition: it is not ζ_K's zero side plus twice L(s, ψ)'s. The explicit formula the kernel names for Z_Q0 (`ZQExplicitFormula`, EpsteinZQ.lean :127-:128) is written over Λ_Q's own zeros, with the coefficients of −Z_Q′/Z_Q in place of von Mangoldt's (`primeTermQ`, :112-:113). The schema it enters and its premises are §2.
