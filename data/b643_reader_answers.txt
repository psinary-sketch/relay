ANSWER 1:
The census calls it "the located clause". It says the Riemann Hypothesis holds exactly when a particular quadratic form is non-negative for every admissible test window k. The form is Q(k) = pole term - prime sum + archimedean term, built from the explicit formula of prime number theory. The admissible windows ("classK") are even, twice continuously differentiable, compactly supported functions of the form h * h~. In the Lean kernel this sign statement is the single Prop h2_sign, which the programme also calls "h2" or "Weil positivity". The compiled theorem h2_sign_iff_rh proves that h2_sign is equivalent to Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis, in both directions and with no hypothesis. The clause is open: it is carried as the programme's one open premise, and the census says plainly that nothing in it claims RH or h2_sign is true. The census also lists two other equivalent "faces" of the same clause: Li positivity and arithmetic-limit positivity. Several things are unclear to me. The census never explains why the clause is called "located". It does not explain what "bounds by its mechanism exclusions" means. And h2_sign, although called the one open premise, does not appear as a row in the v0.7 premise table.

ANSWER 2:
The census defines a HINGE as a premise whose consumers lie in more than one kernel, or in more than one "chain" within a single kernel. A consumer is a theorem whose statement or proof uses the premise, read from the dependency print rather than from the text. A chain is a connected component of a kernel's use-graph, restricted to the declarations that use the premise and named by its terminal declaration. The v0.7 premise table marks 28 of its 50 heads as hinges: AnalyticOnNhd, Continuous, DealignedAt, EpsteinPremises, EqOn, EulerFactorPremise, HCount, HasCompactSupport, HasDerivAt, IdempotentAdd, Integrable, IsExpansion, IsRoot, IsTrivialPoint, NotDiv, PWSetup, PlattTrudgianHeight, Prime, Register4_channelInequality, Register4_positivity, SatisfiesCWeil, SelfDualFE, StepsI, StrictMono, Tendsto, TrivialSummandPremise, ZeroActingPairing and farSmall. Only Prime is a hinge because it spans several kernels (SIDE-carrier-spec, SIDE-explicit-formula and SIDE-grh-transfer). Every other hinge qualifies through several chains inside one kernel. Two things are unclear to me. First, some of the listed chains look like the premise's own witness or refutation theorems (e.g. analyticOnNhd_witness, not_trivialSummandPremise), so I cannot tell whether a premise becomes a hinge partly because of those. Second, the entry "(a cycle) CS" for HasCompactSupport is not explained.

ANSWER 3:
One premise is refuted: TrivialSummandPremise in the SIDE-explicit-formula kernel. Its status is REFUTED-BY-COMPUTATION and its non-vacuity column reads "UNWITNESSED (REFUTED)". The census says it was computed false at b641 and the refutation compiled at b642. The status means the premise as stated is false at an object the programme exhibits, so no witness of it can exist and it is no longer OPEN. The theorem that rests on it is dedekind_rhs, from the Dedekind instance face (F4, v0.25). That theorem is graded INTERFACES on hT : TrivialSummandPremise and also on hE : EulerFactorPremise q. Both dedekind_rhs and not_trivialSummandPremise are listed among the premise's three consumer chains. The census does not say in words what the refutation does to dedekind_rhs. Its grade in the faces table is still INTERFACES (rule), and nothing marks it as changed. From the definitions, a WITNESS is what keeps theorems resting on a premise "not empty". So with no witness possible, dedekind_rhs appears to have a hypothesis nothing can satisfy, which would make it true but empty. That is my reading of the definitions; the census does not state it.
