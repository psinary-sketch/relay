In more recent work K. Maslanka [40] computed λn for 1 ≤n ≤ 3300 and empirically stud-
ied the growth behavior of the Li coeﬃcients. He observed tha t in this range they exhibited
a smoothly growing dominant asymptotic term with superpose d small oscillations exhibiting
some internal structure. The dominant term comes from the ar chimedean prime contribu-
tion S∞(n) in the arithmetic formula above, and the small oscillation s come from the term
Sf (n) represented the ﬁnite places, and |Sf (n)| < 20 over this range. Coﬀey [8],[9] studied
the “arithmetic formula” for the Li coeﬃcients and lower bou nded the archimedean prime
contribution.
The object of this paper is to generalize the Li coeﬃcients to automorphic L-functions,
and to determine their asymptotic behavior as n → ∞ , with or without the assumption of
the Riemann hypothesis. The automorphic L-functions we treat are principal L-functions
over GL(N ) for Q, as given in [10], [29], [31], and [42]. These are (completed ) Langlands
L-functions attached to irreducible cuspidal unitary autom orphic representations appearing
in the right action of GL(n, AQ) on L2(GL(n, Q)\GL (n, AQ)). The associated generalized Li
coeﬃcients will be denoted λn(π).
In §2 we associate to each irreducible cuspidal unitary automor phic representation π an
analogue ξ(s,π ) of the Riemann ξ-function. Our deﬁnition of generalized Li coeﬃcients λn(π)
sets them equal to λn(Z) for the multiset Z =Z(π) of zeros of ξ(s,π ), and we show that these
coeﬃcients λn(π) are well-deﬁned as ∗-convergent series. The multiset Z(π) is invariant under
the transformation ρ ↦→1 − ¯ρ, which implies the symmetry
λ−n(π) = λn(π). (1.9)
It therefore suﬃces to study λn(π) for n ≥ 0. Furthermore the zero sets of the L-functions of a
representationπ and its contragredient representation π∨ are related by complex conjugation
Z(π∨) = Z(π), which implies a second symmetry
λn(π∨) = λn(π). (1.10)
The results of [4] apply to give a Riemann hypothesis criteri on for L(s,π ) in the form: The
Riemann hypothesis holds for L(s,π ) if and only if the real parts ℜ(λn(π)) are nonnegative
for all n ≥ 0.
In §3 we give a “Weil positivity” interpretation of the generali zed Li coeﬃcients. We express
the Weil scalar product associated to the representation π for Li’s test functions in terms of
λn(π). Here the imaginary parts of the λn(π) appear in the scalar products. The test functions
are the same for all representations π.

In §7 we construct for each π an entire functionFπ(z) of order one and exponential type that
interpolates the Li coeﬃcients at integer values, i.e. Fπ(n) = λn(π) for all n ∈ Z. Assuming the

ambiguity concerns zeros at the central critical value s = 1
2 , which require special treatment,
see Theorem 7.1. The polynomial growth of the Li coeﬃcients u nder the RH allows one to
deduce that the function Fπ(z) has a Fourier transform which is well-deﬁned as a tempered
distribution, whose support is a countable closed set in [ −π,π ] having 0 as its only limit point.
This interpolation function Fπ(z) appears to be a new object associated to the zeta zeros. We
do not know any relation of it to various other functions cons tructed from the zeta zeros such
as Cramer’sV -function ([11], [24], [19], [33], [28]) or functions studi ed by Voros [43], [44].
The results of this paper explain some of the empirical obser vations of K. Maslanka [40].
The asymptotic formula (1.16) applied to πtriv, explains the observed behavior of the small
Li coeﬃcients. Since the non-trivial zeros of ζ(s) are known to lie on the critical line up to
heightT ≈ 109 we may expect the ﬁrst 10 16 Li coeﬃcients will also exhibit similar asymptotic
behavior, i.e. the term |Sf (n)| will remain small over this range. Maslanka’s computations of
Sf (n) allow the possibility the term |Sf (n)| is of smaller order of growth than O(√n logn); if
so, this remains to be explained. The approximate formula (1 .17) gives no information on the
precise spectral nature of the “small oscillations” in Sf (n). Perhaps further information can
be extracted from the Fourier transform of the interpolatin g function deﬁned in §7.
