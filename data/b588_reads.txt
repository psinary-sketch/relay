    :111    **Ξ.19 Claim–Certificate Calculus.** *What it is:* the grading instrument that attaches a certificate to every deposited statement and assigns it one of three support grades, so that no line of a manuscript stands without a graded warrant.     :112    
    :113    > ⟨ statement · certificate-type · pin · content-named residue ⟩
    :114    
    :115    where **pin** is the specific machine-checked locus (file · theorem) that discharges the statement, and **content-named residue** is what remains unproven, *named by content* (never counted — see Ξ.23). Assign one of three grades:
    :116    
    :117    - **DERIVES** — the statement follows from a compiled certificate; the pin resolves to a 0-sorry Lean locus.

    :119    - **NOT-COMPILED (shell)** — the statement rests on an outline/stub that *encodes* its own conclusion. A shell is admissible as a work-item, never as evidence.
    :120    
    :121    The five rules:
    :122    1. **No bare claim.** Every claim carries a certificate-type; an uncertified claim is not admissible.
    :123    2. **Pin or downgrade.** The pin must resolve to a specific machine-checked locus; if it does not resolve, the grade drops one step.
    :124    3. **Name, don't count.** A premise may hold INTERFACES grade only if its content is named (Ξ.23); a counted premise ("one thing left") is not named and cannot lift the grade.
    :125    4. **A shell is a work-order, not a citation.** A NOT-COMPILED shell may never be cited as a certificate; it is listed as content-named residue and scheduled as a task.
    :126    5. **Weakest-link monotonicity.** A claim inherits the weakest grade among its premises; no claim rises above its lowest-compiled dependency.

    :305    ## VI. The Five-Door Orientation Object
    :306    
    :307    A method for sustained attack on a single open premise. Where CONSULT scans a claim across all axes, the Five-Door Orientation Object holds one open premise still and decomposes the *approaches* to it into a fixed set of named registers, ordered by how close each is to a machine-checked certificate.
    :308    
    :309    **Full disclosure.** This technique was published-without-filing; it is disclosed here in full as programme method, with no patent claim attached.
    :310    
    :311    **Procedure.**
    :312    1. Take one open premise P (typically the deepest residue from a CONSULT or the top item of a claim's residue list).
    :313    2. Decompose the routes to P into **five named doors** — a fixed set of registers (not five arbitrary ideas, but the five standing approach-classes for that premise). Each door is named by its content (Ξ.23), never numbered as "approach 1..5."
    :314    3. **Order the doors by compiled proximity.** The **nearest** door is the one already holding a *partial machine-checked certificate* (a Lean fragment that discharges part of P). The **farthest** door is one that is honestly *disclaimed with a boundary marker* — a stated reason it does not currently
    :315    4. **Materialize** the ordered doors as a **persistent orientation object** at a **fixed address** (a stable file path), so the object is **inherited across sessions**: a later session opens the same address, sees the same five doors in the same order, and knows without re-derivation which door is n
    :316    
    :317    **Use.** Prevents the two failure modes of long premises: (a) churning approaches without memory across sessions, and (b) presenting the disclaimed farthest door as though it were near. The fixed-address inheritance is the point — the orientation survives the session that produced it.
